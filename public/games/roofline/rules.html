Roofline

Draft bundles of coloured shingles from the lumber yard, stack them on your staging racks, and nail down the best-looking shed roof.

  • Players2–4 (computers fill empty seats)
  • Lengthusually 5 rounds
  • Difficultyeasy to learn
  • Pass and playopen table

Goal of the game

Each roofer shingles a 5 × 5 shed roof. Shingles score more when they touch others in long runs across and down, and roofs earn big bonuses for complete rows, columns and colours. Bundles you can't use fall in the gutter and cost you points.

The game ends after the round in which someone completes a whole row of their roof. Then the highest score wins.

What's in the game

  • 100 shingle bundles: 20 each of Cedar, Slate, Barn Red, Forest and Sand, in a bag.
  • Pallets in the lumber yard: 5 for 2 roofers, 7 for 3 and 9 for 4. Each round every pallet is loaded with 4 bundles from the bag.
  • The yard: the pile where leftover bundles land.
  • The foreman's whistle: decides who starts the next round.
  • A board for each roofer: five staging racks, a roof and a gutter.
  • The scrap bin: used bundles go here. When the bag runs out, the scrap bin is poured back into it.

The roof's colour layout is the same for everyone. Each row and each column holds each colour exactly once, so every colour has exactly one square in every row.

Setup

  1. Empty boards. Every roof, rack and gutter starts empty, and every score starts at 0.
  2. Dormers (on by default): the game picks 3 roof squares at random and marks them ★. They are the same squares on every roof.
  3. Load the yard. Each pallet gets 4 bundles drawn at random from the bag. The yard holds only the whistle.
  4. First roofer. The game picks who starts the first round at random.

How a round works

Roofers take turns in seat order, one take each, until the pallets and the yard are all empty. Then the round ends and everyone nails and scores at the same time.

  1. Take bundles of one colour, either from one pallet or from the yard. You always take every bundle of that colour from that place.
  2. Put them on one rack (or straight into the gutter). Whatever doesn't fit falls into the gutter.
  3. Next roofer. Play passes on until no bundles are left on the pallets or in the yard.
  4. Nail and score. Every full rack nails one shingle onto the roof and scores it. Then gutters are charged and emptied.
  5. Next round, unless someone has completed a roof row. The pallets are refilled and the whistle goes back into the yard.

Taking bundles

From a pallet

Choose a pallet and a colour on it. Take all the bundles of that colour. Every other bundle on that pallet slides into the yard, and the pallet is left empty.

From the yard

Instead of a pallet, you may take all the bundles of one colour from the yard. Nothing else moves.

  • The first roofer to take from the yard in a round also takes the foreman's whistle. It goes into that roofer's next free gutter slot and costs points like a bundle would. If that roofer's gutter is already full, the whistle costs nothing.
  • Whoever took the whistle starts the next round. If nobody took it, the next roofer in seat order after this round's starter begins.

Racks and the gutter

Put everything you took on one of your five racks. Rack 1 holds 1 bundle, rack 2 holds 2, and so on up to rack 5, which holds 5.

  • One colour per rack. A rack that already holds bundles can only take more of the same colour. A full rack can't take any more.
  • No repeats in a row. A rack can't take a colour that is already nailed in its roof row.
  • Overflow. If you take more than the rack has room for, the extra bundles fall into your gutter.
  • Straight to the gutter. You may always put the whole take into the gutter instead, for example when no rack can use it.

End of a round: nailing

When the last bundle has been taken, every roofer does the following:

  1. Nail full racks, top to bottom. Starting with rack 1, each full rack nails one shingle onto its roof row, in the square of its colour. That shingle scores straight away (see Scoring), so shingles nailed earlier this round count as neighbours for later ones.
  2. Clear the rack. The other bundles from a nailed rack go to the scrap bin. Racks that aren't full keep their bundles.
  3. Pay for the gutter. Subtract your gutter total. Your score can't go below 0. Gutter bundles go to the scrap bin; the whistle goes back to the yard.

A run counts every shingle in an unbroken line, including the new one. Diagonal neighbours don't count. A shingle that joins a row of 4 on its own scores 5, not 4 + 1.

End of the game and winning

  • At the end of any round in which at least one roofer has a complete roof row (all 5 squares), the game ends. The rest of that round's nailing and gutter scoring still happens first.
  • Every roofer then adds the roof bonuses: +2 per complete row, +5 per complete column and +8 per complete colour.
  • The highest score wins. On a tie, the tied roofer with the most complete rows wins. If they are still tied, they share the win.

Example round

Your roof already has four shingles around the middle: Sand in row 2, Cedar and Barn Red in row 3, and Forest in row 4. Rack 1 holds one Barn Red and rack 3 holds one Slate.

  1. Your turn. A pallet holds Cedar, Slate, Slate and Sand. You take the 2 Slates onto rack 3, which fills it (3 of 3). The Cedar and Sand slide into the yard.
  2. Later turns. Your gutter ends the round holding the whistle and one Cedar bundle that didn't fit anywhere: 2 slots, −2.
  3. Nailing. Rack 1 is full, so a Barn Red shingle goes onto row 1. Rack 3 is full, so a Slate goes onto row 3, right in the middle.

The Barn Red touches only the Sand below it: a run of 2 down, so 2 points. The Slate joins Cedar and Barn Red across (a run of 3), and Barn Red, Sand and Forest down (a run of 4): 3 + 4 = 7 points. After the gutter, the round is worth 2 + 7 − 2 = +7.

Playing in the Game Center

  • The lumber yard is at the top: the pallets, then the yard with the whistle, the round number and how many bundles are left in the bag. Your board is below it, and the other roofers' boards are beside it.
  • Tap a bundle on a pallet or in the yard to pick up all of that colour there. The racks that can take it light up, and the roof squares it could fill pulse.
  • Tap a rack to put the bundles there, or tap the gutter to drop them in it.
  • Changed your mind? Tap the same bundle again, or press Esc, to put it back.
  • The line at the top says whose turn it is, and the line under it says what the last roofer took. After each round, a message shows what everyone scored and lost.
  • At the end, the results table shows each roofer's rows, columns, colours and total. See roofs hides it so you can look at the boards. The host can tap Play again.
  • Online, friends join with a room code, with table chat and voice. Pass and play works too: everything is on the table, so there are no hidden hands, and each person simply plays at their turn.

Tips and strategy

  1. Keep the small racks busy. Rack 1 fills with a single bundle and can nail a shingle every round. Rack 5 needs five of one colour and can take several rounds to fill.
  2. Build from the middle. A shingle that joins both a row and a column scores twice. Shingles placed in a cross or a block pay far more than scattered ones.
  3. Watch what you leave behind. Taking from a pallet pushes its other bundles into the yard, where the next roofer may get a big haul of one colour.
  4. Count before you take. A take of 4 onto a rack with room for 2 costs you 2 gutter slots. Sometimes a smaller take is worth more.
  5. The whistle is cheap early. Going first next round gives you the first pick of fresh pallets, for −1 if your gutter is empty.
  6. Columns are worth more than rows. +5 against +2. A column needs a shingle from each of your five racks, so plan it across several rounds.
  7. Colours pay +8. A colour needs one shingle in every row. Feeding the same colour to different racks over several rounds builds it.
  8. Know when it ends. A row needs 5 shingles and gets at most one per round, so the game lasts at least 5 rounds. Rack 1 fills so easily that someone usually completes row 1 in round 5. Plan your bonuses for that.
  9. Take the dormers (if on) when they also extend a run. +2 on top of a good run is a big shingle.

Quick reference

Your turn

Take every bundle of one colour from one pallet (the rest go to the yard) or from the yard. Put them all on one rack, or in the gutter.

Racks

Rack n holds n bundles of one colour. No colour that its roof row already has. Overflow goes to the gutter.

The whistle

First into the yard each round takes it: one gutter slot, and that roofer starts the next round.

Round end

Full racks nail 1 shingle each, top to bottom, and score it. Gutter −1, −2, −4, −6, −8, −11, −14. Never below 0.

Shingle score

Alone 1. Otherwise run across (2+) plus run down (2+). Dormer +2.

Game end

After the round someone completes a row. Bonuses: row +2, column +5, colour +8. Tie: most rows wins.

Glossary

Bundle
One shingle tile in one of the five colours.
Dormer
A ★ roof square worth +2 when it's shingled (only with the Dormers option on).
Gutter
Your row of seven penalty slots for bundles you can't or don't use.
Nail
At the end of a round, a full rack puts one shingle on the roof.
Pallet
A wooden pallet in the lumber yard, loaded with 4 bundles each round.
Rack
One of your five staging racks, holding 1 to 5 bundles of a single colour.
Run
An unbroken line of shingles across or down.
Scrap bin
Where used bundles go. It refills the bag when the bag is empty.
Whistle
The foreman's whistle: taken by the first roofer to take from the yard each round. It costs a gutter slot and gives that roofer the first turn of the next round.
Yard
The pile in the middle of the lumber yard where leftover bundles from pallets collect.
