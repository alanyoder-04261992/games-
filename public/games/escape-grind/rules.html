Escape the Grind

A money board game: buy things that pay you every month until your passive income beats your bills, then break out and buy your Dream.

  • Players1–4 (computers fill empty seats)
  • Length40 rounds, 80 rounds or no limit
  • DifficultyEasy to learn; the real-life economy is advanced
  • Pass and playYes, all on one table

Goal of the game

Everyone starts in a job, living paycheck to paycheck on the inner loop of the board, the Grind. Buy rentals, shed lots, storage and businesses that pay you every month. The moment your passive income is bigger than your total monthly expenses, you break out to the outer loop, the Freedom Ring.

On the Freedom Ring you win by buying the Dream on a Dream space you land on, or by adding $50,000 a month of new income from Ventures. If the game has a round limit and nobody has won by then, the player furthest along wins.

What's in the game

The board

The board has two loops of 24 spaces each. You start on the inner loop, the Grind. Only players who have broken out move on the outer loop, the Freedom Ring.

The cards

Jobs

Each player is dealt a different job at random. A job sets your salary, your savings and every bill. Better-paid jobs have bigger bills, so they need more passive income to break free.

Setup

The game always has four seats: Red, Blue, Green and Gold. Seats nobody takes are played by the computer. Each player gets a random job and starts on the Grind's first Payday with:

  • Starting cash = your job's savings + one month of cash flow.
  • No assets, no children and no bank loan, but the debts that come with the job: a home mortgage, a car loan, credit cards, a $1,000 store card and, for jobs that need a degree, a student loan.

A random player goes first, and turns go round the table in seat order.

How a turn works

On the Grind

  1. Sort out your money (optional). Before you roll you may pay off a debt, borrow from the bank or repay the bank, as often as you like. In the real-life economy you may also trade stocks here.
  2. Roll one die. If you gave to charity, you choose one die or two for your next three turns.
  3. Move forward. Every Payday you pass or land on pays your monthly cash flow at once.
  4. Do what your space says: pick a deal, draw a Market or Splurge card, decide whether to give, and so on.
  5. End of turn. Anyone whose passive income is now bigger than their expenses breaks out. Play passes to the next seat; a laid-off player is skipped.

On the Freedom Ring

  1. Trade stocks (optional, real-life economy only). The bank does not lend on the Freedom Ring.
  2. Roll two dice and move. Every Freedom Payday you pass or land on pays your freedom income.
  3. Do what your space says: a Venture, a Dream or a Setback.
A round is one turn for every seat. The round limit in the table options counts these rounds.

Your money: income, expenses and cash flow

Everything you own and owe is on your financial statement. Open yours with the page button (or S, or Y on a controller); tap another player's chip to see theirs.

  • Salary: what your job pays each month.
  • Passive income: the monthly cash flow of everything you own. A rental's cash flow is already after its mortgage payment. Some deals have a negative cash flow; they count against you.
  • Total income = salary + passive income.
  • Total expenses = all your bill lines + the cost of your children + your bank loan payment.
  • Monthly cash flow = total income − total expenses.

Every job's bills are worked out from its salary: taxes 18%, home payment 16%, car payment 4.5%, credit cards 3.5%, groceries, phone and other 15%, a $50 store card, a 3.5% student loan for jobs that need a degree, and 5.5% per child. Each figure is rounded to the nearest $10.

Worked example: one month

If your cash flow is negative, Payday costs you money. If you can't pay a bill on the Grind, the bank lends you what's missing automatically, in $1,000 steps, and that loan raises your expenses.

The Grind spaces

Payday

Collect your monthly cash flow each time you pass or land on a Payday. There are four, six spaces apart, so most trips round the Grind pay you four times.

Opportunity

Choose Small Deal or Big Deal, draw the top card and decide. See Deals.

Market

Draw a Market card. It is a buyer for one kind of asset, a boom that raises income, or a repair bill. See Market cards.

Splurge

Draw a Splurge card and pay its price: from a $140 coffee habit to a $5,000 hot tub. If you're short, the bank lends the rest.

Give

You may give 10% of your total income (salary + passive income). If you do, you choose to roll one die or two on each of your next three turns. Or say "Not now".

New Baby

Congratulations: your expenses go up by your job's per-child cost (5.5% of salary) for the rest of the game. You can have at most three children; after that the space does nothing.

Laid Off

You pay one full month of your total expenses straight away and miss your next two turns.

Deals

On an Opportunity you pick a deck. Only you can buy the card you draw.

  • Small Deals (55 cards) need $1,000 to $7,000 down: starter houses, fixer-uppers, condos, rent-to-own shed lots, backyard storage sheds, small cash businesses, coins, land, and lots of stock cards.
  • Big Deals (18 cards) need $10,000 to $70,000 down: duplexes, 4-plexes, apartment buildings, bigger houses, self-storage, shed dealerships and larger businesses.

Buying a property or business

Pay the down payment in cash and the asset is yours: its cash flow joins your passive income at once. If you don't have enough cash, the screen offers to buy with a bank loan for the difference, rounded up to the next $1,000.

Stocks

Stock cards name a share price for one company: Oakline Lumber (OAK) and Northpeak Tech (NPK) from $1 to $40, Coastal Rail (CRL) from $5 to $30, and the Brightway Fund (BMF) from $10 to $30. The player who drew the card may buy as many shares as they can pay for, or sell shares they own, at that price. Stocks pay no monthly income: you make money only by selling higher than you bought.

Three Small Deal cards are splits: NPK and OAK 2-for-1 (every holder's shares double) and an NPK reverse split (every holder's shares halve, rounded down). They affect everyone at once and end the turn.

Coins and land

Silver coins ($3,000), gold coins ($4,000) and 10 acres of land ($5,000) pay nothing each month, but Market cards may pay 3 times for coins or $30,000 for land.

Market cards

A Market card affects every player still on the Grind, not only the one who landed there. Players on the Freedom Ring ignore it.

  • Buyers. Starting with the player who landed, each Grind player who owns a matching asset may sell any of them. You receive the offer minus the loan still owed on that asset, and its cash flow leaves your passive income.
  • Booms. "Town is booming": every small business (vending, lawn, detailing, car wash, laundromat, pizza, hardware) earns $250 more a month for good. "Backyard storage craze": every shed lot, backyard shed and shed dealership earns $150 more.
  • Repairs. "Burst pipes" ($1,500) and "Tenant skips out" ($800): everyone who owns any rental property (houses, condos, plexes, apartments, storage, shed lots and backyard sheds) pays once.

Loans and paying off debt

Before you roll on the Grind, tap Loans & payoffs:

  • Pay off a debt. Pay a job debt's whole balance in cash and its monthly payment disappears from your expenses. Partial payments aren't allowed. Each debt is a set number of monthly payments: home mortgage 105, student loan 60, car loan 48, credit cards 32; the store card is always $1,000.
  • Borrow $1,000, $5,000, $10,000 or $25,000 from the bank (as many times as you like). Each month the bank loan costs 10% of what you owe (in the real-life economy, the current loan rate), added to your expenses.
  • Repay the bank $1,000, $5,000 or everything you owe.
Paying off debt lowers your expenses, and lower expenses mean you need less passive income to break out. If a payoff or repayment pushes your passive income above your expenses, you break out on the spot.
The bank lends only on the Grind. On the Freedom Ring, a bill you can't pay just takes the cash you have.

Breaking out of the Grind

You break out when your passive income is bigger than your total expenses. Equal is not enough. It is checked at the end of every turn (yours or anyone else's: a boom on another player's turn can free you) and right after you pay off a debt or repay the bank.

When you break out:

  1. Your freedom income is set: 10 × your passive income, rounded to the nearest $1,000, plus $20,000.
  2. You are paid it once straight away, then move to the start of the Freedom Ring (a Freedom Payday).
  3. The Grind no longer touches you: no salary, Grind Paydays, Splurges, babies, Market cards or repairs. Your Grind statement stays as it was; only your freedom income and cash matter now. A layoff ends.

The Freedom Ring

Roll two dice every turn. You can only pay with the cash you have: there are no loans out here.

  • Freedom Payday (5 spaces): collect your freedom income when you pass or land.
  • Venture (10 spaces): draw a Venture card. Pay its price in cash to add its cash flow to your freedom income, or pass.
  • Dream (6 spaces): each Dream space has its own Dream. Pay its price in cash and you win. You can't afford it? You'll be back.
  • Setback (3 spaces): a lawsuit or tax audit takes half your cash; a health scare ($60,000), bad partner ($40,000) or storm damage ($30,000) takes that amount, or all your cash if you have less.

Winning

The game ends the moment someone:

  • buys their Dream on a Dream space, or
  • adds $50,000 a month to the freedom income they broke out with, by buying Ventures.

Out of time. With a round limit (40 or 80 rounds), if nobody has won when the last round ends, the player furthest along wins:

  1. Anyone on the Freedom Ring beats everyone still on the Grind.
  2. Between Freedom Ring players: the most new freedom income added; then the most cash.
  3. Between Grind players: the biggest share of expenses covered by passive income; then the highest net worth.

The end screen has a Summary with everyone's story and final standings by net worth.

The real-life economy (advanced)

Choose Economy: Real-life in the table options. A market index starting at 100 moves once a round, when the first player's turn comes round again. It follows a business cycle, and the gauge at the side of the screen shows the phase, the index and its chart.

The game starts in an Expansion (3 or 4 rounds). Each round the index moves by its phase's amount, give or take up to 3 points of noise, and never leaves 45–180.

What the economy changes

  • Deal prices. Every deal's price and down payment, and every Venture's price, is multiplied by 1 + (index − 100) × 0.009, kept between 0.6 and 1.45. At index 75 that is 0.78: 22% off. The cash flow stays the same. The card is stamped with the sale or the mark-up.
  • Buyers. Market offers are multiplied by the same number: buyers pay more in a boom and less in a recession.
  • Business income. Assets that are businesses (vending, lawn, detailing, car wash, laundromat, pizza, hardware, and shed dealerships) earn the phase's percentage of their cash flow. Rentals, storage and shed lots hold steady. A recession can push your passive income back down.
  • Loan rate. The bank's monthly rate follows the phase, from 5% to 12%.
  • Stock cards show the live market price instead of the printed one.
  • Dream prices never change.

The live stock market

Before you roll, tap Stock market (or the economy gauge) to trade at today's prices: buy 10, 100 or 1,000 shares, sell all of a stock, short 10, 100 or 1,000, or cover a short. You can make up to 8 trades a turn, on the Grind or the Freedom Ring. Prices move every round with the index: NPK swings hardest, then OAK; CRL moves gently and BMF tracks the index.

Short selling

Shorting is a bet that a price will fall. When you cover (close) a short you gain (shorting price − today's price) × shares, or pay the loss if the price rose.

  • You must hold cash of at least 50% of the current value of all your shorts (including the new one) to open a short.
  • You can't short a stock you own: sell it first. You hold one short per stock; cover it before shorting that stock again.
  • Margin call. Each round, if a short's loss is bigger than 80% of your cash, the bank closes it for you and you pay the loss.
In this game opening a short does not pay you the share price up front: you only collect the gain, or pay the loss, when the short closes.

A worked example

Red is a Shed Builder: salary $3,800, expenses $2,210, cash flow $1,590, starting cash $2,490.

  1. Turn 1. Red rolls a 6, passes nothing and lands on the second Payday: +$1,590. Cash $4,080.
  2. Turn 2. Red rolls a 1 onto an Opportunity, picks a Small Deal and draws the Rent-to-Own Shed Lot: $4,000 down. Cash $80, passive income $480, cash flow $2,070.
  3. Turn 3. Red rolls a 1 onto a Market space: "Burst pipes". Red owns rental property and must pay $1,500 with only $80. The bank lends $2,000 (the $1,420 shortfall, rounded up). Cash $580, bank loan $2,000, expenses $2,210 + $200 = $2,410, cash flow $4,280 − $2,410 = $1,870.
  4. Turn 4. Red rolls a 5, passes the third Payday (+$1,870, cash $2,450) and stops on the Opportunity just after it, but passes on the deal. Before the next roll Red repays the $2,000 loan: expenses back to $2,210, cash $450.
  5. Later. More deals stack up. When passive income reaches $2,610 and expenses are $2,370, Red breaks out with $46,000 a month of freedom income and is paid $46,000 at once.
  6. On the Ring. Two dice a turn and five $46,000 Freedom Paydays a lap. With $200,000 saved, Red lands on the Lake Cabin & Dock and buys it: Red wins.

Computer players

  • Easy keeps a small cash cushion, never borrows to buy and mostly ignores the stock market.
  • Normal keeps a safer cushion, repays the bank first, pays off the dearest debts and borrows for good deals.
  • Expert plays like Normal with a bigger cushion and no randomness, reads the business cycle, and shorts the jumpiest stock as the economy cools.

Playing in the Game Center

  • The action bar at the bottom shows your choices as buttons: Roll, Loans & payoffs, Stock market, Small Deal / Big Deal, Buy / Pass, Give / Not now, Sell / Keep them. The highlighted button is the usual choice.
  • Stocks on a deal card: use − and + to choose how many shares, then Buy; or Sell all.
  • Statements: tap a player chip at the top, or the page button, to open a financial statement. Tabs at the top switch players.
  • The 3D table: drag to turn it, pinch or scroll to zoom. Lost the board? Tap the target button or double-tap the table. The camera button cycles views.
  • Keyboard: ←→↑↓ move between buttons, Enter chooses, Esc goes back or passes, S statement, C camera, R centre the board, F full screen, +/− zoom.
  • Game controller: A choose, B back or pass, D-pad or left stick to move, Y statement, X camera, right stick to orbit (click it to centre), LT/RT zoom, Start full screen, View flips through everyone's statements.
  • Real-life economy: the gauge at the side shows the phase, index, deal prices, loan rate and stock prices. When a new phase starts, a "money lesson" explains what it means.
  • Online and pass and play: play with friends online (with chat and voice) or pass one device round; everything is visible on this table, so nothing is hidden between turns. Empty seats are computer players.
  • Game over: Rematch starts a new game with the same players and seats; Summary and Statements show how everyone did.

Tips and strategy

  1. Cash flow beats price. Judge a deal by its monthly cash flow against the cash it takes. A $1,000 backyard shed paying $140 returns 14% a month.
  2. Shed lots are the star small deals. $4,000–$5,000 down for $480–$570 a month.
  3. Don't buy a minus. Negative cash flow deals push your goal further away, unless you are sure a Market buyer will pay well.
  4. Kill the bank loan first. 10% a month is the most expensive money in the game; the store card ($50 on $1,000) is the best payoff after it.
  5. Keep some cash. Splurges, repairs and a layoff all hit at once. A thin cushion saves you a costly bank loan.
  6. Sell into great offers. 2.5× on a shed lot can turn into a Big Deal that pays far more each month.
  7. On the Ring, save for the Dream you'll reach. The first Dream is only two spaces in and costs $200,000. Skip a Venture if buying it would leave you short when you land there.
  8. Real economy: save in the boom, buy in the recession. Rent stays the same while prices drop by up to 40%.
  9. Short the jumpy stock as things cool, and cover once the recession has done its work. Watch your cash: a margin call closes the short at the worst time.

Quick reference

Grind turn

Loans & payoffs (optional) → roll 1 die (2 after giving) → Paydays you pass pay cash flow → do the space → break out if passive > expenses.

Money

Cash flow = salary + passive − expenses. Bank loan: $1,000 steps, 10% a month. Pay off job debts in full to remove their payments.

Grind spaces

Payday: cash flow. Opportunity: Small ($1k–$7k down) or Big ($10k–$70k). Market: buyers, booms, repairs. Splurge: pay. Give: 10% of income for 2 dice ×3. Baby: +5.5% salary. Laid Off: pay expenses, miss 2 turns.

Break out

Passive income > total expenses. Freedom income = passive × 10 (to the nearest $1,000) + $20,000, paid once at once.

Freedom Ring

2 dice. Freedom Payday: freedom income. Venture: buy for more income. Dream: buy to win. Setback: lose half your cash or $30k–$60k. No loans.

Win

Buy a Dream, or add $50,000 a month of freedom income. At the round limit: furthest along wins.

Real-life economy

Expansion → Boom → Slowdown → Recession → Recovery. Prices × (1 + (index − 100) × 0.009). Loans 5–12%. Business income 75–115%. Shorts need 50% cash; margin call above 80% of cash.

Glossary

Asset
Something you own: a rental, shed lot, business, stock, coins or land.
Bank loan
Money borrowed from the bank on the Grind, in $1,000 steps. It costs a monthly percentage of what you owe (10% in the classic economy).
Break out
Leaving the Grind for the Freedom Ring, when passive income is bigger than total expenses.
Cash flow
Total income minus total expenses for one month; paid on each Payday. A deal's cash flow is what it adds to your passive income.
Cover
Closing a short: you collect the fall in price or pay the rise.
Down payment
The cash you pay to buy a deal. The rest of the price is a loan.
Dream
A big goal printed on a Dream space of the Freedom Ring. Buy it to win.
Freedom income
Your monthly income on the Freedom Ring: 10 × passive income at break-out (rounded) + $20,000, plus Ventures.
Grind
The inner loop, where everyone starts living on a salary.
Margin call
The bank closing your short because its loss grew bigger than 80% of your cash.
Market index
In the real-life economy, a number starting at 100 that rises and falls with the business cycle and moves prices.
Net worth
Cash plus what your assets, stocks and ventures are worth, minus all your debts.
Passive income
Money your assets earn each month without your job.
Short
A bet that a stock's price will fall.
Split
A card that doubles (or halves) every holder's shares of one stock.
Venture
A large business on the Freedom Ring that adds to your freedom income.

All jobs, companies, deals and numbers in this game are made up.
