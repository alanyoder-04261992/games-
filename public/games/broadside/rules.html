Broadside

Hide your fleet on a grid, then trade shots across a 3D sea until one fleet is on the bottom.

  • Players2 (you against the computer or a friend)
  • Time10–25 minutes
  • DifficultyEasy to learn
  • Pass and playYes

Goal of the game

You and another admiral each hide a fleet of warships on your own square grid. Then you take turns firing shells at squares in the other admiral’s waters, trying to work out where their ships are.

A ship sinks once every square it covers has been hit. Sink the whole enemy fleet before they sink yours and you win.

What’s in the game

Each admiral has two square grids, called seas, and a fleet of warships. Columns are lettered A, B, C… across the top and rows are numbered 1, 2, 3… down the side, so every square has a name like E5.

The fleet depends on the size of the sea chosen at the table (see Table options). Both admirals always get the same ships.

8 × 8 (quick)Dreadnought 4, Frigate 3, Submarine 3, Patrol Boat 212
10 × 10 (standard)Flagship 5, Dreadnought 4, Frigate 3, Submarine 3, Patrol Boat 217
12 × 12 (long)Flagship 5, Dreadnought 4, Frigate 3, Cutter 3, Submarine 3, Patrol Boat 220

Setup: hiding your fleet

Both admirals place their fleets at the same time, each on their own grid and out of sight of the other. Nobody fires until both fleets are in position.

  • Every ship lies in a straight line, either across or down. No diagonals, no bends.
  • Ships can’t overlap and can’t hang off the edge of the sea.
  • Ships may touch: end to end, side by side or corner to corner are all allowed.
  • You must place every ship in your fleet before you can press Ready.
Ships may touch when you place them yourself. The Shuffle button and the computer admirals always leave a square of water between ships, though. In some paper versions of this game ships may never touch; here they may.

When both fleets are ready, the game picks at random which admiral fires first.

How a turn works

  1. Look at enemy waters. When it’s your turn the camera swings north to the other admiral’s grid. You can see your earlier shots there, but not their ships.
  2. Pick a square and fire. Choose any square you haven’t fired at before. You can never fire at the same square twice.
  3. Hit or miss. If any part of an enemy ship is on that square, it’s a hit: fire breaks out. If not, it’s a miss: a water column rises and a white peg marks the spot. The result is shown to both admirals.
  4. Sunk? If that hit was the last unhit square of a ship, the ship sinks. Everyone is told which ship it was (“You sank their Frigate!”) and the wreck appears on the chart.
  5. Turn passes. In the standard game you get one shot, then it’s the other admiral’s turn. The fire modes below change this.

Hits, misses and sinking

A hit tells you there is a ship on that square, but not which ship. You only learn which ship it was when it sinks. Then its whole outline is shown, so you also know which of your hits belonged to it.

If you have hits that aren’t part of any sunk ship, there is still a damaged ship there. Finish it off before you go hunting elsewhere.

Fire modes

The table picks one of three fire modes before the game. Both admirals play by the same mode.

One shot a turn (default)

You fire one shell, hit or miss, and the turn passes. This is the classic game.

Chain fire: a hit fires again

Every hit earns you another shot straight away, including a hit that sinks a ship. There is no limit: a run of hits can go on and on. Your first miss ends your turn. The banner reminds you: “A hit earns another shot”.

Barrage: a shot per ship afloat

At the start of your turn you get one shot for each of your own ships still afloat. With all five ships on a 10 × 10 sea that’s 5 shots; once you have lost two ships it’s 3. You always get at least one. Hits don’t earn extra shots. The banner counts down: “3 shots left this turn”.

Barrage is like the well-known “Salvo” variant, with one difference: here you fire your shots one at a time and see each result before you aim the next, instead of calling them all at once.

Winning

The moment the last ship of a fleet sinks, the battle ends and the other admiral wins, even in the middle of a chain or a barrage. There are no points: it’s win or lose.

The battle report then shows both admirals’ shots, hits, accuracy (hits as a share of shots) and ships left, with buttons to go back to the Game Center or play a Rematch against the same opponent.

Example: finding and sinking a ship

You are playing one shot a turn on a 10 × 10 sea. These are your shots over five turns (the enemy fires back in between).

  1. D4: miss. Open water.
  2. E5: hit! Fire on the water, but you don’t know which ship it is or which way it lies.
  3. E4: miss. You tried the square above. The ship isn’t lying down through E4.
  4. F5: hit! Two hits side by side in row 5: the ship lies across. It has to carry on to D5 or G5.
  5. G5: hit, and sunk! “You sank their Frigate!” A Frigate is 3 squares long, so E5, F5 and G5 were the whole ship. The wreck is shown and the Frigate is crossed out in their fleet bar.
With Chain fire, step 2’s hit would have given you another shot at once (E4, a miss, ending the turn), and the hits at F5 and G5 would have come in one turn. With Barrage, you might fire all five of these shots in a single turn.

Computer admirals

The computer only knows what you would know: its own fleet, every shot fired, and your ships once they sink. For each open square it counts how many ways your remaining ships could still lie across it, and it always finishes off a ship it has hit.

  • Easy fires at a random square most of the time (7 shots in 10).
  • Normal hunts with the counting method, with an occasional random shot and some guesswork.
  • Expert never guesses: every shot goes where your ships are most likely to be.

Playing in the Game Center

Placing your fleet

  • The ship buttons at the bottom show your fleet. The next ship to place is picked for you; tap a different button to place that one instead. Tapping a ship that is already placed picks it back up.
  • Tap (or click) a square on your grid to drop the picked ship there. A ship can’t go where it would overlap another or run off the edge: you’ll hear a warning sound and see “That spot is taken or off the grid”.
  • Once all ships are down, tap one on the grid to pick it up and move it.
  • Turn the picked ship with ⟳ Across / ⟳ Down or the R key.
  • Shuffle places the whole fleet at random, spread out so no ships touch. Clear takes every ship off. Ready sends your fleet; you can’t move ships after that.

The battle

  • On your turn the camera swings to enemy waters by itself. Tap a square to fire. A shell arcs in, and the result flashes up (“You: F5 — Hit!”).
  • On the other admiral’s turn the camera swings back to your fleet so you can watch their shots land.
  • The camera moves by itself to follow the action. The View my fleet / View enemy waters button and the mini-map switch between the two grids at any time.
  • The ? button in the top bar opens this rule book at any time. In Settings (the gear), High quality graphics turns sea reflections, glowing fire and lights on or off. Turn it off if the game runs slowly on your device.

Playing with people

  • Single player: you against a computer admiral.
  • Multiplayer online: start a table and share the 5-letter code or invite link; your friend joins on their own device. The chat and microphone buttons are in the bar at the top.
  • Pass and play: two people on one device. Choose This device (pass and play) under Multiplayer. Before each admiral’s turn a “Pass to …” screen hides the sea until the right person taps, so neither of you sees the other’s fleet.
  • When the battle ends, Rematch starts a new game with the same players and seats (and the same room code online).

Tips and strategy

  1. Hunt on a checkerboard. While you have no damaged ship to chase, fire at every other square. Every ship is at least two long, so it can’t hide between your shots.
  2. Finish what you start. After a hit, try the four squares next to it. After two hits in a line, keep going along that line both ways until the ship sinks.
  3. Read the sinking message. If you sink a 3-square ship with a run of four hits, the fourth hit belongs to a different ship that is still afloat. Go back for it.
  4. The middle hides more ships. A long ship can lie across a central square in many more ways than across a corner square. Early on, central squares are better bets; once the big ships are gone, the edges matter again.
  5. Use the gaps. If the sea has a gap too small for any ship you still have to find, stop firing into it.
  6. Against the computer, skip the ring. Computer and Shuffle fleets never touch, so the squares all round a sunk ship are always water.
  7. Against people, touch on purpose. Many players skip the squares around a wreck. Hiding two ships side by side, or parking a ship along the edge, can lure them away.
  8. In Barrage, protect your shots. Every ship you lose costs you a shot each turn, so a fleet spread out where it’s hard to find keeps its firepower longer.
  9. In Chain fire, hits snowball. A good hunting pattern pays twice: each hit gives you the next shot for free.

Quick reference

Setup

Hide every ship on your own grid: straight, across or down, no overlaps, nothing off the edge. Touching is allowed. A random admiral fires first.

Your turn

Fire at a square in enemy waters you haven’t tried. Hit = fire, miss = white peg. A ship sinks when all its squares are hit, and its name is announced.

Fire modes

One shot a turn. Chain: keep firing while you hit. Barrage: one shot per ship you have afloat (at least one).

Fleets

8 × 8: 4, 3, 3, 2. 10 × 10: 5, 4, 3, 3, 2. 12 × 12: 5, 4, 3, 3, 3, 2.

Winning

Sink the last enemy ship. The game ends at once.

Controls

Tap to place or fire. R or ⟳ rotates. Mini-map or the dock button switches grids.

Glossary

Admiral
A player. Blue fleet and Red fleet are the two sides.
Afloat
A ship that hasn’t sunk yet, even if it has been hit.
Barrage
Fire mode: one shot a turn for each of your ships still afloat.
Chain fire
Fire mode: every hit earns another shot at once.
Enemy waters
The other admiral’s grid, where you fire. You see only your shots and the ships you have sunk.
Hit
A shot that lands on a square covered by a ship. Marked by a red peg and fire.
Miss
A shot into open water. Marked by a white peg.
Mini-map
The small flat chart in the corner showing the grid you aren’t looking at.
Sea
A square grid: 8 × 8, 10 × 10 or 12 × 12. Each admiral has one with their fleet in it.
Square
One cell of the grid, named by its column letter and row number, like E5.
Sunk
A ship with every square hit. It is shown to both admirals and crossed out in its fleet bar.
