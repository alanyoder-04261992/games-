Ridgeline Rally

Race a hot hatch, a V8 coupe or a mid-engine GT round country roads, against the computer, your friends or your own ghost.

  • Drivers1 alone, or up to 4 online
  • Fieldup to 8 cars
  • Circuits7
  • Race1 to 10 laps
  • Plays withtouch, keyboard, gamepad

Goal of the game

Five red lights come on, they go out, and you race. Be first across the line after the last lap. A lap only counts if you go all the way round the circuit.

In a time trial there is no one else on the road: you chase your own best lap, saved as a see-through ghost car, and try to beat it.

The circuits

There are seven circuits, each a closed loop of country road with its own scenery, light and character. Lengths are for one lap of the centre line.

CircuitLapCharacter

Lakeside Sprint3.1 kmTight corners round a mountain lake at sunset.
Hollow Creek4.0 kmHairpins and a long back straight through the woods, in the morning.
Cedar Pass4.0 kmSwitchbacks and a summit run through tall pines.

Red Mesa4.4 kmFast sweepers in dry red-rock canyon country, late in the day.
Mudline Dirt3.4 kmA dirt stage under cloud: loose surface, dust and four jumps.

Corners have red-and-white kerbs and tyre stacks, and red and white boards on the roadside warn of the big braking zones. Mudline has no kerbs; its jumps are marked by yellow boards and striped posts at the lip.

The cars

Pick one of three cars and one of eight colours. The bars are the ones on the game's car cards.

CarDriveFeel
Sparrow RSfrontA light hot hatch: quick to turn and easy to drive. The best car to learn in.
Brumby V8rearA heavy muscle coupe with the most power. The tail steps out if you stamp on the gas in a corner.
Vela GTrear, mid-engineDownforce grows with speed, so it is the fastest through quick corners.

All three use the same tyre model: weight shifts forward under braking and back under power, and a tyre that is braking or spinning has less grip left for turning. The cars understeer gently at the limit (the nose runs wide) rather than spinning; the Brumby has the least margin. The steering lock narrows as you speed up, so full lock is always the tightest turn the tyres can hold.

Cameras and settings

C (CAM, Y) steps through Chase, Far chase, In car (the driver's seat, with a working wheel and gauges), Bonnet and Bumper; the pause menu has the same list under Camera. The pause menu also has Graphics (Low, Medium, High; High on a computer, Medium on a phone; High adds shadows, soft shading and glow), Corner map, sound volume, and two helpers:

  • Driving assist (on by default on phones): counter-steers real slides and eases the gas when the wheels spin. It leaves a drift you started yourself alone.
  • Auto accelerate (off by default): holds the gas for you, so you only steer and brake.

The HUD

  1. Position and lap: your place in the field (big yellow number) out of the number of cars, and the lap you are on.
  2. Lap time for the lap you are on, and your best lap.
  3. Standings (on a computer): the order of the field, with each car's colour.
  4. Messages: GET READY and the countdown, GO!, each lap's time, Best lap, Final lap!, WIPEOUT!, and the warnings WRONG WAY (red) and OFF TRACK (yellow).
  5. Corner map: the circuit, the yellow start square and every car as a dot in its colour (yours is the biggest; a ghost is see-through white). Top right on a computer, top centre on a phone.
  6. Speedometer: speed in mph, the gear above it (N on the grid, R in reverse) and a rev ring that turns red near the limiter.
  7. Turbo tank: the inner arc. Orange when ready, blue while it burns, faded when there is too little to light it. On a phone the TURBO button fills up the same way.

The race

  1. The grid. Cars line up two by two behind the line. Playing alone you start at the back of the field; online the human drivers start at the front and the computer cars behind.
  2. Lights out. Go. The race clock starts, and lap times run from the start line, so your first lap is timed from when you cross it.
  3. Lap after lap. Each time you cross the line the lap time shows. There are three hidden checkpoints, a quarter, half and three quarters of the way round; a lap counts only if you passed all three. Reversing back over the line takes the lap back.
  4. Final lap! The message comes as you start the last lap.
  5. The flag. Cross the line at the end of the last lap: "Finished 2nd!" The computer takes your car for a cool-down lap and the results follow a few seconds later.

Off the road, wrong way, crashes

  • OFF TRACK appears when you are far from the road. The game never puts you back by itself: when you are far off it, or stuck off it, a yellow ↩ BACK TO THE ROAD button appears. Tap it, or press R (RESET, X on a gamepad) at any time. You are put back on the middle of the road, stopped. Computer cars that get lost are put back on their own.
  • WRONG WAY flashes when you have been driving the wrong way for more than a second.
  • Wipeouts. A big hit (another car side-on, or a tree, above about 30 mph) spins the car and takes the controls away for a moment: WIPEOUT!
  • Rolls and flips. A really bad one rolls or flips the car: a side-on hit above about 47 mph, a tree head-on above about 49 mph, sliding broadside into the rough at about 49 mph, or landing a jump nose-first or very hard. The car tumbles until it stops, then is set back on its wheels right where it is (not back on the road).

Results

The results table lists every car by place: the driver and car, the winner's race time and everyone else's gap behind (in seconds, or laps for a car a lap or more down; "racing" for a car still finishing its last lap), and each car's best lap. The big number at the top is your place; "You win!" if it is 1st. There are no points: the place is the score, and the best lap is the bragging right.

Computer drivers

Computer cars drive all three models in turn, with names like Hank, Ivy and Clem. They follow a racing line that cuts to the inside of each corner, brake for the slowest corner ahead, catch their own slides and move over to pass. They have no turbo. Difficulty sets how close to the limit of grip they drive:

DifficultyEasyNormalHardPro
Share of grip used80 %88 %95 %100 %

Each car also gets a little random spread of its own, so the field strings out.

Time trial

Just you and the clock, lap after lap, with no end. Your best lap on each circuit in each car is saved in this browser as a ghost: a see-through copy of your car that drives that lap again beside you. Beat it and the new lap becomes the ghost.

Leave a time trial with Esc or P (II on a phone) and Back to menu. Each car has its own ghost, so the Sparrow's best lap never races the Vela's.

Scoring and winning

  • Race: the first car to finish the last lap wins. Places follow the order cars cross the line; cars still racing are placed by how far round they are.
  • Lap times run from the line to the line. Your best lap shows on the HUD and in the results, with a "Best lap" message each time you improve it.
  • Time trial: your score is your best lap, and the ghost keeps it.

Example. A 3-lap race on Ridgeline Loop is about 12 km. You finish 2nd, 1.84 s behind Hank: the results show Hank's race time, +1.84 in your Time column and your best lap beside it. A computer car still on its last lap when the results come up shows "racing".

Online play

Choose Multiplayer on the game's tile or options page; friends join with the 5-letter code or the invite link. Up to four people race, each on their own device, and computer cars can fill the field. The host chooses these table options:

  • Everyone picks their own car and colour. If two drivers pick the same colour, the lower seat keeps it and the other gets a free one.
  • The host starts the race for everyone. Human drivers start at the front of the grid. Computer players sitting in the table's seats race too, at their own level (Easy, Normal or Hard).
  • The race ends when every human driver has finished, or 45 seconds after the first car crosses the line. Everyone sees the same results.
  • Rematch on the results screen starts a new race with the same drivers, seats and room code.
  • Time trial is a single-player mode.
Online, the menu doesn't stop the race: it is called the Race menu, and the others keep driving while it is open.

Techniques

The racing line

  1. Brake in a straight line. Brake hard before the corner, while the wheels point straight. The brakes are stronger than the cornering grip (about 1.6 g), and ABS keeps the front wheels turning so you can still steer.
  2. Turn in from the outside edge, easing off the brake as you steer.
  3. Hit the apex. Aim for the inside kerb. On a long corner a late apex (further round) gives a better exit.
  4. Exit on the gas. Unwind the steering as you squeeze the gas, and use all the road.
  • Braking points. Learn one landmark per big corner (the boards, a barn, a tree) and brake at the same place every lap. Brake too late and the nose runs wide; the car never spins on its own, it just won't turn.
  • Don't stamp on it. A tyre that is spinning or braking has less grip left for turning. In the Brumby especially, squeeze the gas out of slow corners or the tail steps out.
  • Stay on the tarmac. The road grips best; the verge less; the grass far less, with more drag. Trees stop you dead.

Drifting

  • Easy drift: going into a corner faster than about 27 mph, tap the brake (shorter than 0.9 s) and get back on the gas within 0.45 s with the steering at least 40 % of the way over. The tail kicks out and the car holds the drift.
  • Handbrake drift: hold Space (or A on a gamepad) above about 18 mph. Not on touch: use the brake tap.
  • Power slide: in the rear-drive Brumby and Vela, too much gas while steering into a slide also starts one.
  • Holding and ending it: steer into the turn for more angle and stay on the gas to keep your speed. Let go of the steering and it settles; steer the other way, or brake hard, and it ends quickly. It will not spin you round.
  • A drift looks great and is good for slow, tight corners and hairpins; on fast sweepers the clean racing line is quicker.

Turbo

Hold TURBO (Shift, RB) for about half a g of extra push. A full tank lasts about 3 seconds and refills by itself in about 9; it needs about 15 % in the tank to light again. Only human drivers have it. Use it out of slow corners and onto long straights, not into a braking zone.

Jumps (Mudline Dirt)

Beating the ghost

  • Watch the gap: if it turns red after a corner, you braked too early or too late there. Try one change per lap.
  • The ghost drives exactly your best lap, so following it shows you your own best line. Then find a few metres it didn't use.
  • A clean lap beats a wild one. One slide too many costs more than a careful corner.

Glossary

ABS
Anti-lock brakes: the wheels keep turning under hard braking, so you can still steer.
Apex
The inside point of a corner that the racing line touches.
Braking zone
The stretch before a corner where you slow down; the big ones have red and white boards.
Checkpoint
One of three hidden points round the lap you must pass for the lap to count.
Downforce
Air pressing the car onto the road at speed; the Vela GT has it.
Drift
A controlled slide with the tail out, the car pointing into the corner.
Ghost
A see-through replay of your best time-trial lap in that car on that circuit.
Grid
The starting positions, two by two behind the line.
Handbrake
A brake on the rear wheels only, used to start a drift.
Kerb
The red-and-white strip on the edge of a corner.
Racing line
The fastest path round the circuit: wide, apex, wide.
Understeer
The front tyres giving up first, so the car runs wide instead of turning.
Verge
The strip beside the road: less grip than tarmac, more than grass.
Wipeout
A spin after a big hit; you lose control for a moment.
