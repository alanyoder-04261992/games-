Spit & Speed

Two real-time card games in one: no turns, both of you play at once, and the fastest hands win.

  • Players2 (you against the computer or a friend)
  • Time2–5 minutes
  • LevelSimple rules, fast hands
  • TurnsNone: real time

Goal of the game

Be the first to get rid of all your cards. You and your opponent race to lay cards onto two shared centre piles, each card one rank higher or lower than the card on top. There are no turns: you both play as fast as you can, at the same time.

The table plays one of two games, picked on the Options page:

  • Speed (the default): play from a hand of five that refills from your draw pile. Empty your hand and your draw pile to win.
  • Spit: play from five stockpiles laid out in front of you. Clear them, race to slap the smaller centre pile, and play rounds until one of you has no cards left.

What’s in the game

One ordinary deck of 52 cards, no jokers. Only the rank of a card matters (Ace, 2 to 10, Jack, Queen, King); suits and colours never do.

You play the red-backed cards and sit at the bottom of the screen. Your opponent plays the blue-backed cards at the top. In Speed the two side piles have green backs. The players are called Red and Blue when they have no name.

Setup

The game shuffles and deals for you.

Speed

  • Each player gets a hand of 5 cards and a face-down draw pile of 15.
  • The last 12 cards make two face-down side piles of 6, one at each end of the middle.
  • The two centre piles start empty. The first “Speed!” turns one card from each side pile onto them.
Speed is played many ways. In this app your hand refills by itself (you never pick cards up), the first flip comes from the side piles of 6 instead of two separate starting cards, and the game ends the instant someone plays their last card: there is no final slap.

Spit

  • Each player gets half the deck, 26 cards.
  • From those you lay out five stockpiles of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 cards (15 cards). Only the top card of each is face up, and both players can see each other’s tops.
  • Your other 11 cards are your face-down spit pile.
  • The two centre piles start empty. Each “Spit!” turns your top spit card onto the pile on your side (the left one, as you sit) and your opponent’s onto theirs.

How the play goes

  1. Get ready. Both players press Speed! (or Spit!). Nothing happens until both have pressed it; the bars at the top and bottom show who is ready.
  2. Flip. One new card lands on each centre pile: from the side piles in Speed, from the spit piles in Spit.
  3. Play, both at once. Lay any of your cards that is one rank up or down from a centre top. Keep going as fast as you can; every card you play changes what fits next, for both of you.
  4. Stuck? When neither player has a card that fits, the game says “Nobody can play.” Both press the button again for a fresh flip.
  5. Repeat until someone is out of cards. In Spit, a round also ends when someone clears their stockpiles: then comes the slap and a new deal.

Playing a card

A card fits on a centre pile if its rank is exactly one step from the top card: a 7 goes on a 6 or an 8. Suit and colour don’t matter. You can play on either pile.

With the default Wrap around rule an Ace counts as both low and high: it goes on a 2 or a King, and a King or a 2 goes on an Ace.

There is no limit to how many cards you can play in a row. A run up or down the ranks is the fastest way to empty your hand:

  • Speed: you play from your hand of five. Each card you play is replaced straight away by the top card of your draw pile, in the same spot. When the draw pile is empty your hand shrinks: 4 cards, 3, and so on.
  • Spit: you play the face-up top card of any stockpile. The card under it then turns face up and can be played next.

Speed and timing: who was first?

Both players act at the same time, so sometimes you both go for the same pile in the same instant. The game settles it simply: moves are taken one at a time, in the order they reach the game.

  • The first card to arrive is checked against the pile and goes down.
  • The second card is checked against the pile as it is now. It was aimed at the old top card, so it usually no longer fits: it stays where it was and you see “Too slow — that pile changed.” If it does still fit the new top, it is played.
  • While the game is answering, the card you sent lifts and fades a little. If it comes back, try again or play something else.
  • Online, every move travels to the host (the player who opened the room) and is taken in the order it gets there, so in a dead heat the move with the shorter trip wins. Against the computer everything happens on your own device.
Speed is about seeing, not just tapping. Look for cards that fit both piles, and keep an eye on the other player’s piles in Spit: their face-up tops tell you what they are about to play.

Stuck: both flip

The play stops when neither player has a card that fits either centre pile. The game notices this by itself and shows the button again: “Nobody can play.”

  • Both players press the button; when the second one does, one new card flips onto each centre pile. It doesn’t matter who presses first.
  • If the new cards still don’t help anyone, the game says “Still stuck — flip again” and you both press again.
  • Speed: if one side pile is empty, both new cards come from the other one. If both are empty, the cards under the two centre tops are shuffled into new side piles first (picture below).
  • Spit: each player spits onto the pile on their side. If one of you has no spit cards left, the other spits onto both piles.
  • Spit, nobody can spit: if neither player can play and neither has a spit card, first anyone who can moves a card into an empty space (next section). If that doesn’t help, each player takes the centre pile on their side and a new round is dealt.

Spit: empty spaces, the slap, new rounds

Empty spaces

When one of your stockpiles is used up, its space stays empty. You can fill it by moving the top card of another stockpile into it, which turns up the card that was underneath.

  • Only a card from a pile with two or more cards can be moved (moving a lone card would gain nothing).
  • You may move cards while playing and while waiting to flip. If a move turns up a card that fits a centre pile while everyone is stuck, play carries on at once without a flip.

The slap

The moment one player plays the last card of their last stockpile, the round stops and both players race to slap a centre pile.

  • Either player may slap, not only the one who cleared their stockpiles.
  • The first slap wins: that player takes the pile they slapped and the other player takes the other pile. A slap that comes too late is turned away (“Too late — that pile is gone”).
  • You want fewer cards, so slap the smaller pile. Slap the bigger one by mistake and you keep it.

The next round

  • Each player gathers their own cards: any stockpile cards still on the table, their spit pile, and the centre pile they took. These are shuffled and dealt into new stockpiles of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, and the rest become the new spit pile.
  • With fewer than 15 cards the stockpiles are dealt as far as the cards go (12 cards make piles of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 2) and there is no spit pile. The other player then spits onto both centre piles.
  • If you clear your stockpiles and have no spit pile, you have played every card: you win at once, no slap.
Spit has many house rules too. In this app either player may slap (the one who cleared their stockpiles gets no head start), a player who runs out of spit cards keeps both centre piles in play while the other player spits onto both, and when nobody can play or spit, each player takes the pile on their side.

Winning

There are no points. The game ends the moment one player has no cards left.

GameYou win when
Speedyou play the last card of your hand with your draw pile already empty (all 20 of your cards).
Spityou clear your last stockpile and have no spit pile left.

Counting cards

Each player’s name bar shows how many cards they have left: hand plus draw pile in Speed, stockpiles plus spit pile in Spit. Each centre pile shows its size in Spit, so you can tell which one to slap. A worked Spit round:

  • You both start with 26: 15 in stockpiles and 11 in the spit pile.
  • After four spits each, you clear all 15 stockpile cards. You have 7 spit cards left. Blue still has 5 stockpile cards and 7 spit cards.
  • The centre holds 15 + 10 cards played and 8 spat: 33 cards, in piles of 13 and 20.
  • You slap the 13. You now have 7 + 13 = 20 cards: 15 in new stockpiles and 5 in your spit pile. Blue has 5 + 7 + 20 = 32. (20 + 32 = 52.)
  • In a later round you slap again and are down to 12 cards: stockpiles of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 2 and no spit pile. Clear them and you win.

When nobody can finish

A few rare cases end the game early, and then the player with fewer cards left wins (equal counts are a draw):

  • Speed, blocked: nobody can play, both side piles are empty and the centre piles have only their top cards, so there is nothing left to flip.
  • About 80 flips in a row with no card played by anyone.
  • Spit: 40 rounds have been played and nobody is out yet.
These tie-breaks are this app’s house rules so a game can never go on for ever. The game-over screen says which one ended it and shows each player’s cards played and cards left.

Example

The Speed table from the setup picture: you hold 3♠ 8♦ Q♥ K♣ 5♣, and the first flip turned up the 9♣ and the 4♥.

  1. The flip. Both players pressed Speed! and the side piles turned up the 9♣ and the 4♥.
  2. You play the 8♦ on the 9♣. Your draw pile slides a 7♠ into the gap.
  3. Blue plays the 5♥ on the 4♥.
  4. You play the 7♠ on your own 8♦, a run downward. A 6♦ fills the gap.
  5. Blue plays the 6♠ on the 5♥. Now your 6♦ fits the 7♠ and your 5♣ fits the 6♠.
  6. You play the 5♣ on the 6♠. At the same moment Blue sends a 7♥ at the 6♠. Your card arrived first, the top is now a 5, and Blue’s 7 bounces back: “Too slow.”
  7. You play the 6♦ on the 7♠. That’s four cards in a few seconds: 16 left for you, 18 for Blue.

Table options

Game

Speed — hand of 5 and a draw pile (default) · Spit — five stockpiles, slap between rounds.

Which of the two games you play.

Ace and King

Wrap around (K–A–2) (default) · Ace is low only.

With wrap, Ace and King are neighbours. Low only: an Ace fits just a 2, and a King just a Queen.

Computer player

The computer’s level is mostly how fast its hands are. Its times vary a little from move to move, and each new flip gives it an extra quarter of a second to look.

Easy (slow hands)

Plays a card about every 1.75 s, presses the flip button after about 1.3 s, slaps after about 1.5 s. About 1 move in 3 is a random one.

Normal

About 1.1 s a card, 0.9 s to press flip, 0.95 s to slap. About 1 move in 10 is a random one.

Lightning

About 0.64 s a card, 0.6 s to press flip, 0.56 s to slap. Never sloppy.

When it plays well, the computer picks the pile that keeps the most of its other cards playable, turns up hidden cards in Spit, and slaps the smaller pile. A “mistake” is a random playable card, or a slap on a random pile. The Game speed setting scales all of its times: Relaxed ×1.6, Normal ×1, Fast ×0.55, Turbo ×0.25. Each player keeps their own clock, so your quick plays don’t slow the computer down or hurry it up.

Playing in the Game Center

  • Tap a card to play it. If it fits both piles, the game puts it on the pile that leaves you the most moves.
  • Drag a card onto the pile you want. The pile lights up gold when the card fits and red when it doesn’t.
  • Spit, empty space: tap a stockpile card that doesn’t fit any centre pile and it moves into your empty space, or drag it onto the space.
  • Speed! / Spit! is the big gold button under the centre piles. After you press it, it reads “Waiting for …” until the other player is ready.
  • Slap by tapping a centre pile when it pulses gold.
  • Keyboard: 1–5 tap your card in that position (left to right), Space or Enter presses Speed!/Spit!, ← → slap the left or right pile.
  • Glow on cards you can play (in the settings, on by default) lifts and lights up every card that fits right now.
  • A card that can’t go anywhere gives a little shake.
  • Online: share the room code; the friend who joins plays Blue. A computer takes any empty seat, and covers for a player who drops out.
  • Game over: the screen shows who won and why, with each player’s cards played and left. Rematch starts a new game with the same players and seats.
Spit & Speed is real time, so there is no pass-and-play mode: each player needs their own device, or play against the computer.

Tips and strategy

  1. Look before you tap. A wrong tap costs a shake; scan both tops, then play.
  2. Chain runs. 6, 7, 8 on the same pile is three plays without looking away. In Speed, watch for the new card that fills each gap: it may continue the run.
  3. Use both piles. If a card fits both piles, put it where your next card fits too. Tapping does this for you.
  4. Block your opponent. In Spit you can see their tops. If they are waiting on a 9, don’t turn a pile into an 8 or a 10 for them when you have a choice.
  5. Press flip at once. When it’s stuck, the round can’t restart until both are ready. A quick press means you see the new cards as soon as possible.
  6. Spit: free the big piles first. Playing from your 4- and 5-card stockpiles turns up more cards, and empty spaces let you dig into them faster.
  7. Slap small. Before the last stockpile card goes, glance at the two pile counts so your hand is already moving to the smaller one.
  8. Hold a spare. In Spit, a lone stockpile card that fits nothing is not wasted: it can wait while you work on the others.

Quick reference

A card fits if

Its rank is one up or one down from a centre top. Any suit. K–A–2 wraps unless the table says Ace is low only.

Speed

Hand 5, draw pile 15, two side piles of 6. Hand refills itself. Empty hand and draw pile to win.

Spit

26 each: stockpiles of 1–5 and a spit pile of 11. Clear the stockpiles, slap the smaller pile, re-deal. No spit pile and no stockpiles left: you win.

Stuck

Nobody can play: both press Speed!/Spit! to flip a card onto each pile. Still stuck: flip again.

Ties

First card to reach the game counts. A card that no longer fits bounces back: “Too slow”.

Controls

Tap or drag a card. 1–5 play, Space flips, ← → slap.

Glossary

Centre piles
The two face-up piles in the middle that both players play onto.
Top card
The card showing on a centre pile. Your card must be one rank away from it.
Wrap
Ace and King count as neighbours (K–A–2). On by default.
Hand
Speed: your five face-up cards at the bottom.

Speed: your 15 face-down cards that refill your hand.

Empty space

The moment both players are ready and one new card lands on each centre pile.
Stuck
Neither player can play any card. Both press the button to flip again.
Too slow
Your card reached the game after the pile had changed and no longer fits, so it stays with you.

Spit: the race to tap a centre pile after someone clears their stockpiles. First slap takes that pile.
