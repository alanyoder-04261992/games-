Liar’s Dice

Roll in secret, bid on what’s under everyone’s cups, and call the bluff before it’s too late.

  • Players2–6 (computer players fill empty seats)
  • Time10–30 minutes
  • DifficultyEasy to learn, hard to master
  • Pass and playYes

Goal of the game

Everyone rolls their dice in secret under a cup. Taking turns, you bid on how many dice of one face are on the whole table, or you call the last bid a lie. Whoever is wrong loses a die.

Lose all your dice and you’re out. The last player with dice left wins.

What’s in the game

Each player has a cup and a set of ordinary six-sided dice: 5 each unless the table chose 3 or 6. Nothing else: no board, no cards, no points.

Setup

Everyone starts with the same number of dice. At the start of every round, all players roll all their dice at once under their cups and look only at their own.

For the first round a player is picked at random to open the bidding. After that, the opener is decided by who lost the last call (see Losing dice).

How a round works

  1. Roll. Everyone shakes their cup and peeks at their own dice.
  2. Opening bid. The opener must bid: a number and a face, like “3 × four”. Any number from 1 up to the number of dice on the table is allowed.
  3. Raise or call. Play passes to the next player still in. They either raise the bid, or call it with Liar! or Spot on. This goes round and round until someone calls.
  4. Lift the cups. Every die on the table is shown, and the dice that count for the bid are added up.
  5. Someone loses a die. Who depends on the call and the count (see below). A player with no dice left is out.
  6. Next round. The player who lost a die rolls for the next round and opens the bidding. Everyone rolls again, one die fewer for whoever lost.

Bidding

A bid is a number and a face: “7 × four” means “there are at least seven fours on the table”, counting every player’s dice, your own included.

Each new bid must be higher than the last one. There are exactly two ways to go higher:

  • Raise the number, on any face: after 4 × three you may bid 5 × two, 5 × six, 9 × four…
  • Keep the number and pick a higher face: after 4 × three you may bid 4 × four, 4 × five or 4 × six.

You can’t bid more dice than there are on the table. Only the player whose turn it is may bid or call.

A bid isn’t a statement about your own dice. You may bid on a face you don’t hold at all: that’s bluffing, and it’s part of the game.

Wild ones

With wild ones (the default), every die showing a one counts as whatever face is being bid. If the bid is on fives, ones count as fives; if it’s on twos, they count as twos. Because ones count for everything, you can’t bid on ones: the faces you can bid are two to six.

With plain ones (a table option), ones are just the lowest face. They count only for a bid on ones, and you may bid on them like any other face.

In some well-known versions (such as Perudo) you may still bid on ones, at about half the number. Not here: with wild ones there are no bids on ones at all. With plain ones, ones are simply the lowest face and follow the normal raise rules.

Calling Liar!

Instead of raising, you may call the last bid a lie: Liar! You can only call the bid made just before your turn. Every cup is lifted and the dice that count for that bid are added up (wild ones included).

  • If there are fewer than the bid said, the bid was a lie: the bidder loses a die.
  • If there are exactly as many or more, the bid was good: you, the caller, lose a die.

Spot on

When the table has Spot on calls switched on (the default), you have a third choice: say the last bid is exactly right. The cups come up and the dice are counted.

  • If the count is exactly the number bid, you were spot on: every other player still in the game loses a die. You keep all yours.
  • If the count is anything else, higher or lower, you lose a die.
This differs from the best-known Spot on rule (“calza” in Perudo), where a correct call wins the caller a die back. Here a correct Spot on call instead costs every other player a die, which makes it a big swing in a crowded game.

Losing dice and going out

Only one die is ever lost per player per call, however far off the bid was.

Who opens the next round: the player who lost a die. When a correct Spot on costs several players a die, the caller opens. If the player who should open has just lost their last die, the next player round the table who still has dice opens instead.

Going out: when you lose your last die, you’re out of the game. Your seat fades and play skips you from then on. If you are playing yourself, you can stay and watch the rest.

Winning

The game ends the moment only one player has dice left. That player wins. There are no points to add up.

One correct Spot on can knock out several players at once: everyone else on their last die is out. The caller never loses a die on a correct Spot on, so there is always a winner.

Example: a full round

Four players, 5 dice each, wild ones and Spot on both on. There are 20 dice on the table. You open the bidding, and play goes You → Ana → Ben → Cy → You. The dice under the cups are:

PlayerDiceCount as fours
You1 3 4 4 63 (4, 4 and a wild 1)
Ana2 4 5 5 61
Ben1 1 3 5 52 (two wild ones)
Cy2 2 3 6 60
  1. You: 3 × four. You can see three that count yourself, so this is safe.
  2. Ana: 4 × five. A higher number, so any face is allowed. Ana has two fives.
  3. Ben: 5 × five. Ben has two fives and two wild ones: four of his own.
  4. Cy: 5 × six. Same number, higher face. Cy has two sixes; a bit of a bluff.
  5. You: 6 × four. A higher number again. You hold three of the six yourself.
  6. Ana: Liar! Ana sees only one four in her own cup and thinks six is too many.

The count: your 4, 4 and 1, Ana’s 4, and Ben’s 1 and 1 make 6. The bid said at least 6, so it was good and Ana loses a die. She now has 4, there are 19 dice on the table, and Ana rolls and opens the next round.

If Ana had said Spot on instead, she’d have been right (exactly 6), and You, Ben and Cy would each have lost a die.

Computer players

A computer player sees exactly what you would: its own dice, how many dice everyone has, and the bids so far. It works out how likely each bid is from its own dice.

  • Easy goes mostly by its own dice, makes rough guesses and bluffs a lot.
  • Normal starts to read the table: a player who bid on fours probably holds some.
  • Expert reads the other bids closely, bluffs less and picks its calls carefully, including Spot on when the odds favour it.

Playing in the Game Center

  • Your dice are in the tray at the bottom; under them a line tells you how many you hold that count for the current bid.
  • The other players sit across the top. Each card shows their cup, a dot for each die they have, their last bid in a speech bubble, and “Out” once they’re gone. A gold edge marks whose turn it is.
  • The current bid is in the middle, with the round number, the number of dice on the table and whether ones are wild.
  • To bid, set the number with − and +, tap a face, then Bid. When you open a round, the number and face start at a sensible bid from your own dice; change them as you like. Liar! and Spot on appear once there’s a bid to call.
  • After a call the cups lift, matching dice glow and the others fade, a red “−1 die” or “Out!” badge marks who lost, and the middle says how many there were. The player who opens next taps 🎲 Roll for the next round.
  • Keyboard: ↑ ↓ change the number, ← → or 1–6 pick a face, Enter bids and L calls Liar!
  • Show the odds (in Settings, on by default) shows the chance a bid is true. It works from your own dice only, treating every hidden die as random; it can’t see anyone else’s cup and doesn’t guess what their bids give away.
  • Online: start a table under Multiplayer and share the 5-letter code or invite link; chat and voice chat are in the bar at the top. Pass and play: pick This device (pass and play) and type a name for each person. Before each person’s turn a “Pass to …” screen hides the dice until they tap.
  • When the game ends, Rematch starts a new game with the same players and seats.

Tips and strategy

  1. Count your own dice first. They’re the only ones you know for sure. With wild ones, remember to count your ones for every face.
  2. Add a share of the rest. With wild ones about a third of the hidden dice will count for any face; with plain ones about a sixth. Your dice plus that share is a fair bid.
  3. Open low and honest. A modest opening bid on your strongest face is hard to call and gives little away.
  4. Listen to the bids. A player who keeps raising fives probably holds fives. Bids carry information, true or not.
  5. Raise on a face you hold. When the bid comes round, moving to a face you’re strong in makes your bid safer and sends the others off guessing.
  6. A tie favours the bidder. Call Liar! only when you think the count will fall short; if it’s exactly right, you lose.
  7. Bluff with care. A bid on a face you don’t hold can push the next player into a bad raise, but if they call, it’s your die.
  8. Spot on is a weapon late in the game. With few dice left, exact counts are easier to judge, and a right call can knock several players out at once.
  9. Watch the dice total. As dice disappear, the same bid gets riskier: 6 × four is ordinary with 20 dice, bold with 12.

Quick reference

Each round

Everyone rolls in secret. The opener bids. In turn, each player raises or calls. One die is lost; the loser opens the next round.

A legal raise

More dice on any face, or the same number on a higher face. Never more than the dice on the table. With wild ones, no bids on ones.

Liar!

Count < bid: bidder loses a die. Count ≥ bid: caller loses a die.

Spot on

Count = bid: every other player loses a die. Otherwise the caller loses one.

Wild ones

A one counts as the face being bid (default). Plain ones count only as ones.

Winning

No dice left = out. Last player with dice wins.

Glossary

Bid
A claim that at least so many dice on the whole table show a face, like “7 × four”.
Bluff
A bid you don’t believe, made to mislead the others.
Call
Ending the bidding with Liar! or Spot on. Everyone’s dice are then shown and counted.
Count
How many dice on the table actually match the bid’s face, wild ones included.
Cup
What hides your dice from the other players during a round.
Face
The number of pips on a die, one to six.
Liar!
A call that the last bid is too high.
Opener
The player who makes the first bid of a round.
Out
A player who has lost all their dice.
Raise
A new bid that beats the last one: more dice, or the same number on a higher face.
Spot on
A call that the last bid is exactly right.
Wild one
With the default rule, a one counts as whatever face is being bid.
