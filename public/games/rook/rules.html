Rook

A partnership trick-taking game: bid for the nest, name trump with your first lead, and take the counting cards before the other team does.

  • Players4, in two teams (computer players fill empty seats)
  • Time30–45 minutes to 1000
  • LevelMedium · ages 10+
  • Pass and playYes, with hidden hands

Goal of the game

You and your partner sit across from each other and play as a team against the other two. Each round, the teams bid for the right to pick up the nest and set trump, then play ten tricks to capture the cards that count points.

The first team to reach 1000 points wins the game. If you win the bid you must take at least as many points as you bid, or you lose the whole bid.

What’s in the game

Rook uses its own deck, not ordinary playing cards. There are no suits: there are four colours, red, green, yellow and black, plus one special card, the Bird.

Each colour has 11 cards: 1 and 5 to 14. There are no 2s, 3s or 4s. That makes 44 coloured cards and the Bird: 45 cards in all.

Cards that count

Only 21 of the 45 cards are worth points. The rest are worth nothing, but they still win tricks.

Setup: the deal

The game picks a dealer at random. The dealer shuffles and deals 10 cards to each player and puts the last 5 cards face down in the middle. Those 5 cards are the nest.

  • Partners sit across from each other. In the app you are always at the bottom (South) and your partner is at the top (North).
  • Dealing, bidding and play all go clockwise, and the deal moves one seat clockwise every round.
  • If anyone is dealt a hand with no point cards at all, the cards are shuffled and dealt again by the same dealer.

How a round works

  1. Deal. 10 cards each and 5 in the nest.
  2. Bid. Starting left of the dealer, players bid how many points their team will take, or pass. The highest bidder wins.
  3. Take the nest and bury. The high bidder picks up the 5 nest cards, then buries any 5 cards face down. (A 400 or 800 bid changes this step; see Shooting the moon.)
  4. Lead and set trump. The high bidder leads the first trick. The colour of that first card becomes trump.
  5. Play 10 tricks. Follow the colour led if you can. The highest trump wins the trick, or the highest card of the colour led if no trump was played. The winner leads the next trick.
  6. Score. Count the points each team took, add 20 and the buried cards for whoever won the last trick, and check whether the bidding team made its bid.
  7. Next round. Everyone taps Continue, the deal moves one seat clockwise, and you play again until a team reaches 1000.

Bidding

A bid is a promise: “our team will take at least this many points this round.” The winner of the bidding gets the nest and leads first, which also means choosing trump.

  • The player to the left of the dealer bids first, then bidding goes clockwise.
  • Bids start at 100 and go up in steps of 5, up to 200. Each bid must be higher than the last one. You may jump straight to any higher bid.
  • Instead of bidding you can pass. Once you pass you are out of the bidding for the rest of the round.
  • You may bid over your own partner.
  • Bidding ends when everyone except the high bidder has passed.
  • If the first three players all pass, the dealer is stuck and must bid (at least 100).
A jump bid (say from 110 straight to 140) tells your partner you have a strong hand. There are 200 points in a round, so 100 is about half and 200 means taking everything.

Notice that the other team may win a trick and still lose the fight: the bid only fails if they collect at least one point, and every 5, 10, 14, 1, the Bird and the last trick are points.

The nest and burying

After a normal bid (100 to 200), the high bidder picks up the 5 nest cards, so they briefly hold 15. They then bury 5 cards face down to get back to 10. Nobody else sees the nest or the buried cards.

  • You may bury any cards, point cards included.
  • Points you bury are not lost: they go to the team that wins the last trick, along with its 20 points.
  • A good bury empties a colour (a void), so you can trump it later, and keeps your long colour for trump.

Trump and the Bird

In this version nobody announces trump. Instead, the high bidder’s first lead names trump: whatever colour that card is becomes trump for the whole round.

  • Because trump is set by that first card, the first trick is always a trump trick: everyone must follow it with trump if they can.
  • Until trump is set, the Bird belongs to no colour. From then on it is a trump card.
  • Leading the Bird first lets the bidder name any of the four colours.
In many printed versions the bidder names trump after looking at the nest. Here the first lead sets it, as in the original app. Where the Bird ranks also varies between families: this table puts it between the 10 and the 11 unless the host picks “highest” or “lowest” in the Bird option.

Playing the tricks

A trick is one card from each player, played in turn clockwise. The high bidder leads the first trick; after that, whoever won the last trick leads.

  1. Lead. The leader may play any card.
  2. Follow. Everyone else must play the colour that was led if they have it. When trump is led, the Bird counts as trump and must be played if it is your only trump.
  3. Can’t follow? Play any card. Playing a trump is called trumping, and it can win the trick.
  4. Who wins. The highest trump played wins. If no trump was played, the highest card of the colour led wins. Cards of other colours never win.
The Bird can’t be used to escape following. If another colour is led and you still hold that colour, you must play it, not the Bird.

Scoring

When all the tricks are played, each team counts the point cards in the tricks it won. The team that won the last trick adds 20 plus the points in the buried cards.

Worked arithmetic

  • Made: you bid 140. Your tricks hold 125 points, you win the last trick (+20) and the buried cards hold 5. You took 125 + 20 + 5 = 150. 150 ≥ 140, so you score +150. The other team took 200 − 150 = 50 and scores +50.
  • Set: same bid of 140, but your tricks hold only 110 and you win the last trick: 110 + 20 + 5 = 135. That’s short of 140, so you score −140. The other team scores its 65.
  • Solo: you bid 400 and the other team wins one trick holding a single 5. They took 5 points, so you score −400 and they score +5.

Scores can go below zero.

Winning the game

After each round the scores are added up. As soon as a team has 1000 or more, the game ends and that team wins. (The Play to option can set 500 or 1500 instead.)

  • If both teams reach the target in the same round, the higher score wins.
  • If both reach it with exactly the same score, play another round.

Example hand

East deals, so you (South) bid first. You hold four good greens (14, 12, 10, 9) and open at 120. West bids 125. Your partner North jumps to 140. East passes, you pass, West passes: North takes the bid at 140 (see the bidding picture above).

  1. The nest. North picks up red 13, red 5, green 5, yellow 7 and the Bird, and buries green 5, green 6, yellow 7, yellow 11 and black 9 (5 points buried). North now has no green and no yellow.
  2. Trump. North leads the red 1. Red is trump, so everyone must play red if they can. Nothing beats the 1, so North wins the trick.
  3. Drawing trump. North leads red 14 and red 13 to pull out the other players’ trumps.
  4. A trump-in. Later you lead green 14, but East still has the green 1. North, with no green, trumps with the red 8 and wins, so your green 14 and East’s green 1 (25 points) go to your team. This is the trick in the “Playing the tricks” picture.
  5. The last trick. North keeps the red 12 for the end and wins the last trick: +20, plus the 5 buried points.

North and South took 125 in tricks + 20 + 5 = 150, beating their 140 bid: +150. East and West took the other 50. The score is 150 to 50. The deal moves one seat clockwise, from East to you, so next round you deal and West bids first.

Host settings (gear button, during the game)

  • Game speed: Relaxed, Normal (default), Fast or Turbo: how long computer players wait before moving.
  • Computer players: Easy, Normal (default) or Expert. Easy bids and plays loosely; Normal and Expert think ahead by trying out many possible deals, and Expert tries the most.
  • Play out sure tricks automatically: off by default. When it is on and the player to lead is certain to win every remaining trick, the rest of the round plays itself.

Playing in the Game Center

Your turn

  • Bidding: use − and + (steps of 5) or +10 / +20 to set your bid, then tap Bid, or tap Pass. The 400 and 800 buttons under “Shoot the moon” ask you to confirm first. When the dealer is stuck, the Pass button disappears.
  • Burying: tap cards to raise them (up to the number shown, like 0/5), tap a raised card to put it back, then tap Bury. Suggest lets the coach choose; Clear starts over. The panel shows how many points you are burying.
  • Handing over a card (your partner bid 400): tap one card, then Hand it over.
  • Playing: tap a card, or drag it up onto the table. Cards you can’t play are dimmed, and the prompt tells you what to do (“Follow red”, “No green — play anything”). Leading the Bird first opens the trump picker.
  • Keyboard: 1 to 9 and 0 play the 1st to 10th card in your hand, counting from the left. Enter or Space continues after a round.

Helpers

  • Coach (light bulb): suggests a bid, picks the cards to bury, or highlights a good card to play. Turn on “Show suggestions (coach)” in Settings to keep the highlight on.
  • Tricks won: during play, tap the trick count under a team’s score (or the check-mark stack button) to see every card in the tricks each team took this round. Last trick shows the trick just played.
  • Score sheet: every round so far, with bids and scores.
  • Settings (gear): Auto-play when only one card is legal (off by default), Auto-continue after each round (on), Show suggestions (off), Large cards, and the 3D table (on; turn it off on slow phones). Autopilot lets the computer play your seat until you take over.

Playing with people

  • Single player: you and three computer players.
  • Multiplayer online: share the room code. The first friend to join sits across from you as your partner. Empty seats are filled by computer players.
  • Pass and play: several people on one device. Between turns a “Pass to …” screen hides the hands until the next person taps.
  • Rematch: at the end of the game, Rematch starts a new game with the same players, seats and room code.

Tips and strategy

  1. Count before you bid. Long colours and high cards win tricks. The nest holds about 20 points on average (5 of the 45 cards), but you can’t count on what’s in it.
  2. Think about your first lead. It sets trump, so lead from your longest, strongest colour. Leading its 1 sets trump and usually wins the trick too.
  3. Pull trump early. As the bidder, lead trump until the other team has none left, then cash your winners in the other colours.
  4. Bury to make voids. Emptying a short colour lets you trump it. Bury point cards only if you couldn’t protect them anyway; you get them back by winning the last trick.
  5. Count trumps. There are 12 trumps (11 and the Bird; 9 with the short deck). Keep track of how many have fallen.
  6. Feed your partner. When your partner is sure to win a trick, throw on your 10s, 14s and 5s. When the other team is winning, throw your worthless cards.
  7. Save something for the end. The last trick is worth 20 plus the buried points, often enough to make or break a bid.
  8. Defend to set. A set costs the bidders their whole bid, so a missed 150 is a 150-point swing plus everything you take.
  9. Shoot the moon only with a lock. One stray 5, or losing the last trick, ruins a 400. The 800 has no nest to help you.

Quick reference

Deal

10 cards each, 5 in the nest (7 each with the 33-card deck). Redeal if a hand has no points.

Bid

100 to 200 in 5s, or 400 / 800 solo. Pass and you’re out. Dealer is stuck if three pass.

Nest

Bidder takes the 5 and buries 5. Buried points go to the last trick’s winner.

Trump

The bidder’s first lead sets trump. Lead the Bird to name any colour.

Rank

5 low … 14, 1 high. Bird between 10 and 11 of trump (option).

Play

Follow the colour led if you can, else anything. Highest trump wins, else highest of the colour led.

Points

5s = 5, 10s and 14s = 10, 1s = 15, Bird = 20, last trick = 20. 200 a round.

Score

Made: score what you took. Set: lose the bid. Other team scores what it took. Solo: ±400 / ±800.

Win

First to 1000 (or 500 / 1500). Both over: higher score wins; a tie plays on.

Glossary

Bid
The number of points your team promises to take this round.
Bird
The special blue card, worth 20 points. Once trump is set it is a trump card.
Bury
To put cards face down after taking the nest. Buried points go to whoever wins the last trick.
Colour
One of the four groups of cards: red, green, yellow, black. Like a suit in other games.
Counters
The cards worth points: 5s, 10s, 14s, 1s and the Bird.
Dealer is stuck
When the first three players pass, the dealer must bid.
Follow
Play a card of the colour that was led.
Lead
Play the first card of a trick.
Nest
The 5 cards dealt face down in the middle, taken by the high bidder.
Set
Missing your bid. The bid is subtracted from your score.
Shoot the moon
A solo bid of 400 or 800. You play alone and the other team must take no points.
Sitting out
The solo bidder’s partner, who doesn’t play that round.
Trick
One card from each player; the highest wins all of them.
Trump
The colour that beats all the others for the round, set by the bidder’s first lead.
Void
Having no cards of a colour, so you may trump when it is led.
