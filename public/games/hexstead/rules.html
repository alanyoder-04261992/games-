Hexstead

Settle a hex island: harvest timber, brick, wool, grain and stone, trade with your neighbours, and raise homesteads and barns until you reach the target score.

  • Players3–8 (computers fill empty seats)
  • Lengthplay to 8, 10 or 12 points
  • Difficultymedium
  • Pass and playhidden hands

Goal of the game

Everyone starts with two homesteads on the corners of an island made of hex tiles. Each turn the dice decide which tiles produce resources. You spend those resources on trails, more homesteads, barns and almanac cards, and each of them moves you toward the target score.

The first player to reach the target score on their own turn wins. The target is 10 points unless the table chose 8 or 12.

What's in the game

The island is made of hex tiles. Five kinds of land each produce one resource, and the Badlands produce nothing. Every tile except the Badlands carries a number token from 2 to 12. The game lays out a new island every time it starts. It never puts a 6 next to an 8 (or 6 next to 6, or 8 next to 8), and it tries to keep matching numbers apart.

The number tokens are 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 8, 8, 9, 9, 10, 10, 11, 11 and 12. There is no 7 token: a 7 is when the bandit acts.

  • Resource cards: the bank holds 19 of each resource on the standard island. What you hold is private; the others only see how many cards you have.
  • Almanac cards: a deck of 25 face-down cards you can buy (see Almanac cards).
  • Harbors: 9 signs around the coast of the standard island. Four are 3:1 harbors and five are 2:1 harbors, one for each resource.
  • The bandit: blocks the tile it stands on.
  • Two awards, worth 2 points each: Longest Trail and Big Posse.

Setup

The game picks a first player at random. Then everyone places pieces for free in a "snake" order: forward round the table, then back again.

  1. Place a homestead on any free corner of a tile. The distance rule applies: no homestead may touch a corner right next to another one.
  2. Place a trail on one of the edges touching the homestead you just placed.
  3. Pass to the next player. When the last player has placed, that player places a second homestead and trail at once, and the order runs back to the first player.
  4. Collect starting resources. Starting with the fifth homestead placed, each homestead pays its owner one card for every tile it touches (a coast corner may touch only one or two tiles; the Badlands pay nothing).
With 4 players, the "fifth homestead on" rule means every player's second homestead pays, as in the picture. The game counts placements, not rounds, so it works out differently at other table sizes. With 3 players, the third player's second homestead (the 4th placed) pays nothing; only the 5th and 6th pay. With 5 to 8 players, the players who place 5th and later in round 1 are paid for their first homestead as well as their second.

After setup, the first player takes the first real turn. Everyone starts with 2 points (two homesteads).

How a turn works

  1. Roll the dice. Every tile showing the number rolled produces. Each homestead on its corners collects 1 card of that tile's resource, and each barn collects 2. This pays every player with a building there, not just you. The tile under the bandit produces nothing. A 7 pays nothing and wakes the bandit (see Sevens and the bandit). You may play a Sheriff before you roll.
  2. Trade with the other players or the bank, as often as you like (up to 3 offers to the table per turn).
  3. Build and buy: trails, homesteads, barns and almanac cards, as many as you can pay for. You may also play one almanac card this turn.
  4. End your turn. Play passes to the next player. With 5 or more players, barn raising comes first.

After rolling, steps 2 and 3 can be mixed in any order: trade, build, trade again, play a card, build some more.

When the bank runs short. If the bank doesn't have enough of a resource to pay everyone owed it on a roll, nobody gets that resource. If only one player is owed it, that player gets whatever the bank has left.

Trails

A trail goes on an edge between two tiles (or along the coast). It must touch one of your homesteads or barns, or one of your trails. A trail can't go through someone else's building: if an opponent has built on a corner, your trail that ends there doesn't connect to anything on the far side.

Homesteads

A new homestead goes on a free corner that one of your trails touches. It must also follow the distance rule: none of the three neighbouring corners may hold a homestead or barn, yours or anyone's.

Barns

A barn replaces one of your homesteads. It is worth 2 points instead of 1 and collects 2 cards instead of 1. The homestead goes back into your supply, so you can build it again somewhere else.

You can't build a new homestead if all 5 are on the board. Upgrade one to a barn to get it back. Once your 4 barns are out, the rest stay homesteads.

Sevens and the bandit

  1. Discard. Every player, the roller included, who holds more than 7 resource cards gives half of them (rounded down) back to the bank. Each player picks their own cards. Almanac cards don't count.
  2. Move the bandit. The roller moves the bandit to any other tile. It can't stay where it is, and it may go to the Badlands.
  3. Rob. If any opponents with at least one resource card have a building on that tile's corners, the roller must pick one of them and takes one of their resource cards at random. If there's no one to rob there, nothing is taken.

The tile under the bandit produces nothing until the bandit moves again, whatever number is rolled.

Friendly bandit (a table option): the bandit can't be sent to a tile where any opponent with 2 points or fewer has a building. Only points everyone can see count; hidden Landmarks don't. If that rules out every tile, the bandit may go anywhere.

Trading and harbors

You can trade only during your own turn, after you roll. There are two ways to trade.

With the other players

Make an offer to the whole table: some of your cards for some of theirs, for example "1 Stone and 1 Wool for 1 Brick". You can't ask for a resource you're also giving.

  • Every other player answers yes or no. A player who doesn't hold the cards you asked for is counted as a no automatically.
  • If one or more say yes, you choose which one to trade with, or none. If everyone says no, the offer lapses.
  • You may withdraw your offer at any time.
  • You can make up to 3 offers per turn. Players can't make counter-offers, and they can't trade with each other when it isn't their turn.

With the bank

  • The bank trades 4 of one resource for 1 of any other, as often as you like.
  • A harbor is yours once you have a homestead or barn on either of its two pier corners. A 3:1 harbor lowers the rate to 3 for every resource. A 2:1 harbor lowers it to 2 for its own resource only.
  • You always get the best rate you hold for each resource, and the bank must have the card you ask for.

Almanac cards

  • Buying a card costs 1 Wool, 1 Grain and 1 Stone. You draw the top card of the face-down deck. Only you see what you bought.
  • You may play one almanac card per turn, and not one you bought this turn.
  • Sheriff can be played before you roll or after. You move the bandit and rob exactly as on a 7, but nobody discards. If you play it before rolling, you roll afterwards as normal.
  • Trailblazer, Bumper Crop and Corner the Market are played after you roll. Trailblazer's trails follow the usual trail rules, and you must place both of them if you have room. Bumper Crop's two resources may be the same kind, if the bank has them.
  • Landmark is never played. It counts 1 point for you as soon as you buy it, and stays hidden from the others until the game ends.

Longest Trail and Big Posse

Longest Trail (2 points) goes to the first player with an unbroken run of at least 5 trails. Only the longest single path counts: branches don't add up, and a run stops at a corner with an opponent's building on it.

  • Another player takes the award only by building a longer run. Tying the holder isn't enough.
  • If the holder's run is cut and someone else is now strictly longest (5 or more), the award moves to them. If two or more players tie for longest, or nobody has 5 any more, nobody holds it until someone pulls ahead.

Big Posse (2 points) goes to the first player who has played 3 Sheriffs. Another player takes it only by playing more Sheriffs than the holder.

Barn raising

With 5 or more players, a lot happens between your turns. So when a player ends their turn, each other player in seat order gets a short building turn:

  • You may build trails, homesteads and barns, and buy almanac cards. You can't trade or play almanac cards.
  • Tap Done when you're finished. Players who can't afford anything are skipped automatically, so it goes quickly.
  • A card you buy during barn raising can be played on your next turn.
  • Barn raising doesn't win the game straight away. If it takes you to the target, you win as soon as your own turn starts.

Barn raising is on by default for 5 or more players and can be turned off in the table options. It never happens with 3 or 4 players.

  • You win the moment you reach the target during your own turn. That can happen after a roll, a build, a card or an award. You don't need to finish or end the turn.
  • Points you gain during someone else's turn (an award changing hands, or barn raising) don't end the game then. If you still have the target when your turn begins, you win at once.
  • Only one player can be taking a turn, so there are no ties.
  • The others see your points without your Landmarks. Your own panel shows them as, for example, 7+1.

Example: a full turn

Red has 2 homesteads and 1 barn (4 points) and holds 1 Timber, 1 Brick, 0 Wool, 2 Grain, 4 Stone.

  1. Roll. Red rolls 4 + 5 = 9. Red's homestead on a Fields 9 collects 1 Grain (Grain 3). Blue's homestead on a Pasture 9 collects 1 Wool.
  2. Plan. Red wants a homestead (Timber, Brick, Wool, Grain) and a barn (2 Grain, 3 Stone). That needs 3 Grain, 3 Stone and a Wool. Red has 3 Grain and 4 Stone, but no Wool.
  3. Trade. A bank trade would cost 4 Stone for 1 Wool, leaving too little Stone for the barn. Red offers the table 1 Stone for 1 Wool. Blue has a spare Wool and accepts, Ivory declines, and Red taps Trade. Red now has 1 Timber, 1 Brick, 1 Wool, 3 Grain, 3 Stone.
  4. Build a homestead. Red taps Homestead, picks a glowing corner at the end of a trail and pays 1 Timber, 1 Brick, 1 Wool, 1 Grain. That's 5 points; Red has 2 Grain and 3 Stone left.
  5. Upgrade to a barn. Red taps Barn, picks a homestead on a busy 6 and pays 2 Grain + 3 Stone. A homestead (1) becomes a barn (2): 6 points. Red's hand is now empty, safe from the next 7.
  6. End turn. Red ends with 6 points and a hand the bandit can't touch.

A harbor trade

Ivory has a homestead on a 2:1 Grain harbor and holds 5 Grain. Ivory can make two bank trades: 2 Grain for 1 Brick and 2 Grain for 1 Timber. That leaves 1 Grain and makes a trail. At the plain 4:1 rate, the same 5 Grain would buy only one card.

Playing in the Game Center

The board

  • Drag to turn the island; pinch or scroll to zoom. The circular-arrow button on the right resets the view.
  • The list button opens the table log (every roll, build and trade). The house button shows the building costs.
  • Player panels across the top show each player's points, resource cards, almanac cards, Sheriffs played and longest trail, plus badges for the awards.

Your turn

  • Tap Roll dice. If you hold a Sheriff, a Sheriff first button lets you play it before rolling.
  • Tap Trail, Homestead or Barn in the build bar, then tap a glowing spot on the island. Each button shows its cost, and a cost chip gets a red outline when you're short. Almanac buys a card straight away.
  • On a phone or tablet, the first tap picks a spot and shows the piece there. Tap the same spot again to build. A near miss finds the closest spot, and a two-finger pinch never builds anything.
  • Trade opens two tabs. Bank & harbors shows your rate for each resource (3:1 and 2:1 rates are highlighted). Players has + and − steppers to build an offer to the table.
  • Tap an almanac card in your hand to play it. Cards marked new can be played from your next turn.
  • Pop-ups handle discards on a 7, choosing whom to rob, Bumper Crop, Corner the Market and answering offers.
  • End turn passes play on. In barn raising, tap Done.

Settings, online play and rematch

  • Settings can turn on a low-graphics mode for older devices, decline offers from computer players automatically, and show or hide the table log.
  • Online, friends join with a room code, and the table has chat and voice. Empty seats are taken by computer players.
  • Pass and play: several people can share one device. A "Pass to …" screen hides each hand between people.
  • At the end, Final scores lists everyone's homesteads, barns, awards and Landmarks. Rematch starts a new game with the same players and seats.

Tips and strategy

  1. Count the dots. Each dot on a token is one way in 36 to roll that number. A corner touching 6, 8 and 5 has 5 + 5 + 4 = 14 dots, so it pays far more often than one touching 2, 12 and 11 (4 dots).
  2. Start with timber and brick. Trails and homesteads both need them, and early expansion matters most.
  3. Plan for barns. Grain and stone build barns, which double a homestead's income and add a point. A good grain or stone spot pays off all game.
  4. Pick a harbor to match. A 2:1 harbor for a resource you produce a lot turns a surplus into anything you need.
  5. Watch the 7. Seven is the most common roll (6 in 36). Try to end your turn with 7 or fewer cards, or spend them on an almanac card.
  6. Play a Sheriff before rolling when the bandit sits on your best tile. Your tile is free again before the dice roll.
  7. Corner the Market is strongest right after a roll that paid one resource to many players.
  8. Block on purpose. A homestead on a corner in the middle of a rival's trail cuts it, and it can take Longest Trail away from them.
  9. Count hidden cards. A player who has bought several almanac cards may hold Landmarks and be closer to winning than their score shows.
  10. Barn raising (5+ players): resources you keep can be spent between turns. That cuts both ways: a full hand is still at risk on a 7.

Quick reference

On your turn

(Sheriff) → Roll → trade, build, buy, play 1 card in any order → End turn. 5+ players: barn raising, then the next turn.

Costs

Trail: Timber + Brick
Homestead: Timber + Brick + Wool + Grain
Barn: 2 Grain + 3 Stone
Almanac: Wool + Grain + Stone

Production

Matching number: homestead 1, barn 2. Bandit tile: nothing. Bank short with several owed: nobody gets it.

Rolling a 7

More than 7 cards: discard half (round down). Move the bandit to a new tile. Rob one random card from an opponent there.

Placement

Trails connect to your pieces, never through an opponent's building. Homesteads touch your trail and have no building on a neighbouring corner.

Trading

Your turn, after rolling. Up to 3 table offers. Bank 4:1, 3:1 harbor, 2:1 harbor for its resource.

Almanac cards

1 per turn, not on the turn you bought it. Sheriff can go before the roll. Landmarks just count.

Points

Homestead 1 · Barn 2 · Longest Trail (5+) 2 · Big Posse (3 Sheriffs) 2 · Landmark 1. Reach the target on your own turn to win.

Glossary

Almanac card
A card bought from the deck for Wool, Grain and Stone: Sheriff, Trailblazer, Bumper Crop, Corner the Market or Landmark.
Badlands
The sandy tile with no number token. It produces nothing, and the bandit starts there.
Bandit
The figure in a black hat. It blocks the tile it stands on and robs when it moves (on a 7 or a Sheriff).
Bank
The supply of resource cards. It pays out on rolls and trades 4:1, or better with a harbor.
Barn
An upgraded homestead: 2 points, collects 2 cards.
Barn raising
With 5+ players, the short building turns everyone else gets after a turn ends.
Big Posse
The 2-point award for the first player to play 3 Sheriffs.
Corner
A point where tiles meet. Homesteads and barns sit on corners.
Distance rule
No building may sit on a corner right next to another building.
Edge
A side of a tile. Trails run along edges.
Harbor
A sign on the coast with two pier corners. Building on one gives you a 3:1 or 2:1 bank rate.
Homestead
Your basic building: 1 point, collects 1 card.
Landmark
A hidden almanac card worth 1 point.
Longest Trail
The 2-point award for the longest unbroken run of 5 or more trails.
Number token
The disc on a tile. When its number is rolled, the tile produces. The dots show how likely it is.
Target score
The points needed to win: 8, 10 or 12.
Trail
A piece on an edge that connects your buildings and leads to new corners.
