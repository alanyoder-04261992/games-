How to play Pinochle

Players

Four players in two teams. Partners sit across from each other. Dealing, bidding and play go clockwise, and the deal passes to the left each hand.

The deck

48 cards: two each of the Ace, 10, King, Queen, Jack and 9 in all four suits. Cards rank, high to low: A, 10, K, Q, J, 9 — the 10 beats the King! When two identical cards land in one trick, the one played first wins. Everyone gets 12 cards.

Bidding

  • The player left of the dealer bids first. A bid is how many points your team promises to score from meld and tricks together.
  • Bids start at the opening bid (250 unless the table chose otherwise) and go up in 10s. You can bid over your partner.
  • Once you pass, you're out for this hand. Bidding ends when only one player is left.
  • If the first three players pass, the dealer is stuck with the opening bid.

Trump and passing

The winning bidder names trump. Then (unless the table plays without passing) the bidder's partner passes 4 cards to the bidder, and the bidder passes 4 back. Partners usually send trump and Aces; the bidder sends back cards that don't help the meld.

Meld

Everyone lays down their meld for all to see. A card may be used in different kinds of meld, but not twice in the same kind.

The run already includes its King and Queen — a royal marriage counts only with another King and Queen of trump.

After seeing the meld, the bidder may throw in the hand instead of playing it: the bidding team loses its bid and the other team scores just its meld.

Playing the tricks

  • The bidder leads the first trick; the winner of each trick leads the next.
  • You must follow suit. If you can't, you must play a trump. If you have neither, play anything.
  • You must also beat the winning card whenever you can (head the trick) — even if your partner is winning. If the trick has been trumped and you're following suit, you don't have to.
  • The highest trump wins; otherwise the highest card of the suit led.

Scoring

  • Counters: every Ace, 10 and King you take is worth 10. The last trick is worth 10 more. There are 250 in each hand.
  • A team must take at least one trick to keep its meld.
  • If the bidding team's meld plus counters reaches the bid, it scores all of it. If not, it is set and loses the amount of the bid.
  • The other team always scores its counters (plus its meld if it took a trick).
  • The first team to 1500 wins. If both teams pass 1500 in the same hand, the bidding team wins.
