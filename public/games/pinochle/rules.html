Pinochle

Four-hand partnership Pinochle: bid on your hand, name trump, swap cards with your partner, show your meld, then fight for the Aces, 10s and Kings.

  • Players4, in two teams (computer players fill empty seats)
  • Time30–60 minutes to 1500
  • LevelMedium to hard · ages 12+
  • Pass and playYes, with hidden hands

Goal of the game

You and your partner, sitting across from each other, play as a team. Each hand your team scores points two ways: by showing meld (special combinations of cards in your hand) and by winning counters (Aces, 10s and Kings) in tricks.

The first team to 1500 points wins. The team that wins the bidding must make its bid with meld and counters together, or it loses the whole bid.

What’s in the game

There are no cards below 9. Because every card comes twice, two players can play the very same card in one trick, and you can hold doubles for bigger meld.

Setup: the deal

The game picks a dealer at random. The dealer deals all 48 cards, 12 to each player. There is no kitty or widow. Each new hand the deal moves one seat clockwise.

In the app your cards are sorted by suit, high cards first. Once trump is named, the trump suit moves to the left end of your hand.

How a hand works

  1. Deal. 12 cards each.
  2. Bid. Starting left of the dealer, players bid how many points their team will make from meld and counters together, or pass. The highest bidder wins.
  3. Name trump. The winning bidder picks the trump suit.
  4. Pass cards. The bidder’s partner passes 4 cards to the bidder, and the bidder passes 4 back.
  5. Meld. Everyone shows their meld and it is counted. The bidder may throw in the hand instead of playing it.
  6. Play 12 tricks. The bidder leads first. Follow suit, trump if you can’t, and beat the winning card whenever you can.
  7. Score. Each team adds its meld (if it won a trick) and its counters. The bidding team must reach its bid or lose it.

Bidding

A bid is how many points your team promises to score this hand from meld plus counters. You only see your own cards, so you bid on your own meld plus what you hope your partner can add.

  • The player to the left of the dealer bids first, then bidding goes clockwise.
  • The first bid is at least the opening bid: 250 unless the table chose 200 or 300. Each bid after that must be higher, in steps of 10. You may jump.
  • Once you pass you are out of the bidding for this hand. You may bid over your partner.
  • Bidding ends when everyone except the high bidder has passed.
  • If the first three players all pass, the dealer is stuck: there is no Pass button and the dealer must bid at least the opening bid.

Trump and passing

The winning bidder names any suit as trump. Trump cards beat every card of the other suits, and trump decides which meld counts as a run, a royal marriage or a dix.

Then partners help each other. With the default table, the bidder’s partner passes 4 cards to the bidder first. The bidder then passes 4 cards back; these can include cards just received. The other team doesn’t pass.

As the partner, send the bidder trump (especially cards that fill a run) and Aces. As the bidder, send back cards that aren’t in your meld, and avoid sending counters the other team could catch.

Meld

After passing, every player lays down their meld for everyone to see. The app finds and counts all of it for you, then everyone picks their cards back up and plays the hand. The meld sheet shows each player’s meld and cards.

Using a card twice

There are three kinds of meld: trump meld (runs, royal marriages, dix), arounds, and marriages and pinochles. A card can count once in each kind, but never twice in the same kind.

  • A run counts once (150). With a second King and Queen of trump you add a royal marriage: 150 + 40 = 190. A single extra King, without a Queen, adds nothing.
  • A double around or double pinochle replaces the single one: two of every Ace is 1000, not 1000 + 100.
  • The Queen of spades can be in a marriage and in a pinochle at the same time, as in the picture.

Keeping your meld

Meld isn’t safe yet: a team’s meld only counts if that team wins at least one trick during the play.

Throwing in the hand

After seeing everyone’s meld, the bidder may throw in the hand instead of playing it. The bidding team loses the amount of its bid, and the other team scores only its meld (no tricks are played). This saves the other team’s counters when the bid is hopeless anyway.

This table follows common four-hand partnership rules. A few details you may know differently: meld is saved by winning any trick (some groups need a trick with a counter in it); a throw-in gives the other team its meld only; and there is no redeal for a hand of nines.

Playing the tricks

The bidder leads the first trick. After that, whoever won the last trick leads. Each player plays one card, clockwise.

  1. Follow suit. If you have the suit that was led, you must play it.
  2. Head the trick. When you follow suit, you must play a card that beats the winning card if you have one, even when your partner is winning. If the trick has already been trumped (and trump wasn’t led), any card of the suit is fine.
  3. Can’t follow? Trump. If you have none of the suit led, you must play a trump. If a trump is already winning, you must beat it if you can.
  4. Neither? With no cards of the suit led and no trump, play any card.
  5. Who wins. The highest trump wins. If no trump was played, the highest card of the suit led wins. If two identical cards tie for best, the one played first wins.
Heading the trick is compulsory, even when your partner is already winning it. If the only card you hold that beats the winning card is your Ace, you must play the Ace; you can’t keep it back for later.

Scoring

When all 12 tricks are played, each team counts its counters: 10 for every Ace, 10 and King in the tricks it won, plus 10 for the last trick. That makes 250 in every hand.

Worked arithmetic

  • Made: you bid 300. Your team melds 250 and takes 160 in counters: 250 + 160 = 410. That reaches 300, so you score +410. The other team melded 100 and took the other 90 counters: +190.
  • Set: same bid and meld, but you take only 40 in counters: 250 + 40 = 290, short of 300. You score −300. The other team scores 100 + 210 = +310.
  • Thrown in: you throw in instead: you score −300, and the other team scores only its meld, +100.
  • No trick: if the other team had won no tricks at all, it would have no counters and its 100 meld would be lost, so it would score 0.

Scores can go below zero.

Winning the game

  • The first team to reach 1500 (or 1000 or 2500, if the table chose) at the end of a hand wins.
  • If both teams reach the target in the same hand, the team that won the bid that hand wins, even if the other team has more points.

Example hand

East deals, so you (South) bid first. You hold the 10, King and Queen of hearts and a pinochle (Queen of spades, Jack of diamonds), and you hope North can help. Bidding goes as in the bidding picture: you 250, West 260, North pass, East 270, you 300, West and East pass. You win the bid at 300.

  1. Trump. You name hearts. The trump buttons showed hearts would give you the most meld.
  2. Passing. North passes you A♥, J♥, A♣ and 10♣. Those hearts complete your run. You pass back 9♣, J♣, 9♠ and 9♦, none of which are in your meld.
  3. Meld. You show a run in hearts (150) and a pinochle (40): 190. North shows Queens around: 60. Your team has 250. West has Kings around (80) and East a marriage in diamonds (20): their team has 100. The meld sheet says you need 50 from the tricks.
  4. Play. You lead your trumps from the top to pull the other players’ trumps, then cash your Aces. Your team takes 160 in counters, including 10 for the last trick. East and West take the other 90.

Your team: 250 meld + 160 counters = 410, which beats 300, so +410. Theirs: 100 + 90 = +190. Next hand you deal, because the deal moves clockwise from East to South, and West bids first.

Host settings (gear button, during the game)

  • Game speed: Relaxed, Normal (default), Fast or Turbo: how long computer players wait before moving.
  • Computer players: Easy, Normal (default) or Expert. Easy often plays a random legal card and bids less carefully; Expert looks ahead when choosing which card to play.

Playing in the Game Center

Your turn

  • Bidding: the panel shows your best meld and the suit that gives it. Use − and + (steps of 10) to set your bid, then tap Bid, or tap Pass.
  • Naming trump: tap one of the four suit buttons. Each shows the meld you’d have and how many cards of that suit you hold.
  • Passing: tap cards to pick them (the panel counts, like 2/4), tap again to put one back, then tap Pass 4. Suggest picks cards for you. Cards you receive are highlighted.
  • Meld: read the meld sheet and tap Play the hand. If you’re the bidder you can tap Throw in the hand instead (it asks you to confirm).
  • Playing: tap a card. Cards you can’t play are dimmed; tapping one tells you why (“Follow spades — and beat the winning card if you can”).
  • After each hand the score sheet shows meld, counters and the score; tap Next hand. “All hands” lists every hand so far.

Helpers (Settings, gear button)

  • Auto-play when only one card is legal: on by default.
  • Auto-continue after meld and each hand: off by default. (The bidder always decides about throwing in.)
  • Show suggestions (coach): off by default. When on, the coach suggests a bid, marks the best trump suit and highlights a good card to play.
  • Large cards, and Autopilot, which lets the computer play your seat until you take over.
  • Last trick (under the bid during play) shows the trick just played.

Playing with people

  • Single player: you and three computer players.
  • Multiplayer online: share the room code. The first friend to join sits across from you as your partner. Empty seats get computer players.
  • Pass and play: several people on one device. Between turns a “Pass to …” screen hides the hands until the next person taps.
  • Rematch: at the end of the game, Rematch starts a new game with the same players, seats and room code.

Tips and strategy

  1. Bid on meld you can see, plus some. Count your best meld (the panel shows it), add about 20 to 30 for help from your partner’s pass and meld, and expect your team to win somewhere around half of the 250 counters.
  2. Bidding tells your partner things. Bidding when you could pass usually says “I have meld or a long suit”. Use it to compete even when you don’t want the bid.
  3. Pass for the run. As the partner, pass trump first (Aces and 10s of trump fill runs), then off-suit Aces.
  4. Pass back safely. As the bidder, pass back cards outside your meld, and keep counters you can win yourself.
  5. Check the meld sheet. It tells you exactly how many counters you need. If it says you can’t make it, throwing in saves the other team’s counters.
  6. Pull trump. As the bidder, lead trump early so your Aces in other suits aren’t trumped.
  7. Remember the head-the-trick rule. Opponents must beat your card if they can, so leading a high card can force their high cards out.
  8. Feed counters to the winner. When your partner is sure to win a trick, throw on your 10s and Kings; when the other team is winning, play your 9s, Jacks and Queens.
  9. Always win one trick. A team that wins no tricks loses its meld, so make sure you get at least one.
  10. Save a winner for the end. The last trick is worth 10 extra.

Quick reference

Deal

48 cards (A, 10, K, Q, J, 9 twice in each suit). 12 each.

Bid

From 250 (option) in 10s. Pass and you’re out. Dealer is stuck if three pass.

Trump and pass

Bidder names trump. Partner passes 4 to the bidder, bidder passes 4 back (option).

Meld

Run 150 · royal marriage 40 · dix 10 · arounds A 100, K 80, Q 60, J 40 · marriage 20 · pinochle 40 · doubles 1500 / 1000 / 800 / 600 / 400 / 300.

Play

Follow suit and beat the winning card if you can; else trump (beat a winning trump if you can); else anything. First of two equal cards wins.

Count

A, 10, K = 10 each; last trick 10. 250 a hand.

Score

Meld counts only with a trick won. Made: meld + counters. Set: lose the bid. Throw in: lose the bid, they score meld only.

Win

First to 1500 (or 1000 / 2500). Both over in one hand: the bidding team wins.

Glossary

Around
Four cards of the same rank, one in each suit: Aces, Kings, Queens or Jacks around.
Bid
The number of points (meld plus counters) your team promises to make this hand.
Counters
Aces, 10s and Kings, worth 10 each when you win them in tricks. Also the points they make.
Dealer is stuck
When the first three players pass, the dealer must bid.
Dix
The 9 of trump, worth 10 as meld.
Double
Both copies of a meld: double run, double pinochle, double Aces around.
Head the trick
Play a card that beats the card winning the trick. Required whenever you can.
Marriage
King and Queen of the same suit. A royal marriage is in trump.
Meld
Card combinations you show for points before the tricks are played.
Pinochle
The Queen of spades and the Jack of diamonds together.
Run
A, 10, K, Q, J of trump. Also called a flush.
Set
Missing your bid. The bid is subtracted from your score.
Throw in
The bidder gives up the hand after the meld, losing the bid without playing.
Trump
The suit named by the bidder; any trump beats any card of another suit.
