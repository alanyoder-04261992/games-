How to play Euchre

Players

Four players in two teams of two. Partners sit across from each other. Play goes clockwise, and the deal moves one seat to the left after every hand.

The deck

24 cards: the 9, 10, Jack, Queen, King and Ace of each suit. Everyone gets five cards. The other four make the kitty, and its top card is turned face up.

Trump and the bowers

Once trump is chosen, the two Jacks of trump's color are the best cards in the deck:

  • Right bower — the Jack of trump. The highest card.
  • Left bower — the other Jack of the same color. It counts as a trump, not as its own suit.

Trump ranks, high to low: right bower, left bower, A, K, Q, 10, 9. The other suits rank A, K, Q, J, 10, 9.

Example: Hearts are trump. The Jack of Hearts is the highest card, the Jack of Diamonds is second — and it's a heart for this hand. Diamonds then have only five cards: A, K, Q, 10, 9.

Choosing trump

  • Round one. Starting left of the dealer, each player may order up the turned card (its suit becomes trump) or pass. Even the dealer's opponents can order it up. When it's ordered up the dealer picks the card up and discards one card face down.
  • Round two. If everyone passes, the card is turned face down. Going around again, each player may name any other suit as trump, or pass.
  • If everyone passes twice, the cards are thrown in and the next player deals. With stick the dealer (a table setting), the dealer can't pass in round two and must name a suit.

The team that chose trump are the makers; the other team are the defenders.

Going alone

Whoever chooses trump can go alone: their partner puts their cards down and sits the hand out. Take all five tricks alone and your team scores 4 points. (If the dealer's partner goes alone, the dealer sits out and the turned card isn't picked up.)

Playing the hand

  • The player left of the dealer leads first (skipping anyone sitting out).
  • You must follow the suit that was led if you can. Remember the left bower belongs to trump.
  • If you can't follow, play anything — a trump wins the trick, or throw away a low card.
  • The highest trump wins; with no trump, the highest card of the suit led wins. The winner leads next.

Scoring

Makers take 3 or 4 tricks1 point
Makers take all 5 (a march)2 points
Alone and take all 54 points
Alone and take 3 or 41 point
Makers take fewer than 3 (euchred)2 points to the defenders

The first team to 10 points wins (5 or 15 if you set the table that way).

Tips

  • Three trumps including a bower is usually enough to call. Off-suit aces help a lot.
  • Think about who gets the turned card: ordering up gives it to the dealer — great if that's your partner, risky if it's an opponent.
  • If your team made trump, lead trump to pull the other side's trumps.
  • Don't trump your partner's winning card. Throw your lowest card instead.
  • The coach (light bulb) suggests a move whenever it's your turn.
