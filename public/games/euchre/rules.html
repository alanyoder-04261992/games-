Euchre

A fast partnership trick-taking game: name trump, ride the two bowers, and be the first team to 10 points.

  • Players4, in two teams
  • Time15–25 minutes
  • LevelEasy to learn
  • Cards24 (9 to Ace)
  • Pass and playYes, with hidden hands

Goal of the game

You play with a partner who sits across from you, against the other two players. Each hand, one team chooses the trump suit and then has to win at least three of the five tricks. Win them and your team scores; fail and the other team scores instead.

The first team to reach 10 points wins the game (5 or 15 if the table is set that way).

What’s in the game

Euchre uses a short deck of 24 cards: the 9, 10, Jack, Queen, King and Ace of each of the four suits. There are no jokers and no 2s to 8s.

There is no board and nothing to count during play: each hand is just five tricks. The game keeps the score for you at the top of the table (Us and Them).

Setup

  • Seats. Four players: you (South), West, your partner North, and East. Partners sit across from each other, so the seats alternate: you, an opponent, your partner, an opponent.
  • The first dealer is picked at random. After every hand the deal moves one seat to the left (clockwise), even after a hand that nobody wanted to play.
  • The deal. Everyone gets five cards. The four cards left over make the kitty, and its top card is turned face up. That card is the up-card: its suit is offered as trump first.

How a hand works

  1. Deal. Five cards each, four to the kitty, and the up-card turned face up.
  2. Round one of bidding. Starting with the player on the dealer’s left and going clockwise, each player either passes or orders up the up-card. Ordering up makes the up-card’s suit trump. The dealer is the last to speak and can take it too (“pick it up”).
  3. The dealer’s swap. If someone ordered it up, the dealer adds the up-card to their hand and puts one card face down on the kitty. It can be any card, even the one just picked up.
  4. Round two of bidding (only if all four passed). The up-card is turned face down. Going round again from the dealer’s left, each player may name any other suit as trump, or pass.
  5. Nobody wants it? If all four pass in round two, the cards are thrown in and the next player deals a new hand. (With the stick the dealer option the dealer can’t pass in round two and must name a suit.)
  6. Play five tricks. The player on the dealer’s left leads the first trick. Everyone must follow the suit that was led if they can. The best card wins the trick, and the winner leads the next one.
  7. Score. After five tricks, count the makers’ tricks: 3 or more and the makers score, fewer than 3 and the defenders score. Then the deal moves left.
The team that chose trump are the makers; the other two are the defenders. It doesn’t matter who ordered it up: if your partner calls trump, you are a maker too.

Trump and the bowers

Trump is the strongest suit for the hand: any trump beats any card of another suit. Euchre adds a twist: the two Jacks of trump’s colour become the two best trumps.

  • Right bower: the Jack of the trump suit. The highest card in the game.
  • Left bower: the other Jack of the same colour. The second-highest card, and for this hand it belongs to the trump suit, not to the suit printed on it.

After the two bowers, trumps rank Ace, King, Queen, 10, 9. So trump has seven cards, the suit that lost its Jack has only five, and the other two suits rank normally: A, K, Q, J, 10, 9.

The left bower changes suit. If hearts are trump, the J♦ is a heart: you can’t play it to follow a diamond lead, and you must play it (or another heart) when hearts are led and you have no other heart.

Choosing trump

Round one: order it up, or pass

Everyone looks at the up-card. On your turn, say Order it up if you think your team can take three tricks with that suit as trump, or Pass. You can order it up even when the dealer is an opponent: they still get the card.

The dealer gets the up-card (unless the dealer’s partner goes alone), so think about who that is. If your partner is dealing, ordering up puts an extra trump in your team’s hands. If an opponent is dealing, you are handing them a trump.

Round two: name a suit, or pass

If all four players pass in round one, the up-card is turned face down. That suit is now out: it can’t be trump this hand. Going round again from the dealer’s left, each player may name one of the other three suits, or pass.

If everyone passes again the hand is thrown in, nobody scores, and the next player deals. With Stick the dealer switched on, the dealer has no Pass button in round two and has to name a suit, so every hand gets played.

Going alone

Whoever orders up or names trump may go alone. Their partner lays their cards down and sits the hand out, and the caller plays against both opponents with only their own five cards. Going alone is worth it only if you expect to take all five tricks: that scores 4 points instead of 2.

  • Only the player who calls trump can go alone, in round one or round two. The defenders can’t.
  • Every trick has three cards. Play skips the partner who is sitting out, including on the first lead.
  • If the dealer’s partner orders up and goes alone, the dealer is the one sitting out, so nobody picks up the up-card. It stays on the kitty and its suit is still trump.
  • If the dealer orders up (picks it up) alone, the dealer takes the card and discards as usual and their partner sits out.
  • Taking three or four tricks alone scores just 1 point. Taking fewer than three gets you euchred like any other maker.
Going alone can be switched off in the table options. It is allowed by default.

Playing the tricks

  1. The lead. The player on the dealer’s left leads the first trick (if that player is sitting out, the next player leads). You may lead any card.
  2. Follow suit. Going clockwise, each player must play a card of the suit that was led if they have one. Remember the left bower counts as trump.
  3. Can’t follow? Then you may play any card: a trump to try to win the trick, or a low card you don’t need.
  4. Who wins. If any trumps were played, the highest trump wins. If not, the highest card of the suit that was led wins. A card of another suit that isn’t trump can never win.
  5. Next trick. The winner collects the trick and leads the next one. After five tricks the hand is scored.
In this app you can’t play a wrong card by mistake: cards you aren’t allowed to play are darkened and can’t be tapped. That is why there is no penalty for “reneging” (failing to follow suit), which some tables use.

Worked scoring

  • Your partner orders up and your team takes 4 tricks: +1 for you. The defenders score nothing for their one trick.
  • You name clubs and your team takes all five: a march, +2.
  • You go alone and take all five: +4. If you had dropped one trick it would have been only +1.
  • West names diamonds but their team takes only 2 tricks: they are euchred and your team scores +2.
  • A game to 10: the score is Us 7, Them 9. They call trump and you euchre them: Us 9, Them 9. Next hand you march: Us 11, and you win.

Winning. The score is checked after every hand. As soon as a team has reached the target (10 by default) the game ends and that team wins. Going past the target is fine: 11 wins a game to 10.

Some tables give 4 points for euchring a player who went alone, or let a defender go alone too. This app keeps it simple: a euchre is always 2 points, and only the caller can go alone.

Example hand

East is dealing, so you (South) speak first. You hold the hand below and the up-card is the 10♥.

  1. You order it up. With hearts as trump you have the left bower, the A♥ and the K♥, plus the A♠. That’s a strong hand, even though the 10♥ goes to East, an opponent. Hearts are trump and your team are the makers.
  2. East picks up the 10♥ and discards the 10♠ face down. You lead first, because you sit on the dealer’s left.
  3. Trick 1. You lead the 9♣. West plays the 10♣, your partner wins with the A♣, and East follows with the J♣. Us 1.
  4. Trick 2. North leads the K♣ and East plays the Q♣. You have no clubs left. West still has to play and is out of clubs too, so West could trump North’s King. You trump it yourself with the K♥, higher than any trump West could play. West throws the K♠. Us 2.
  5. Trick 3. You lead the A♥ to draw out trumps. West plays the 9♥, North has no hearts and throws the J♠, and East wins with the right bower, J♥. Them 1.
  6. Trick 4. East leads the 10♥. You win it with the left bower, J♦, and West’s Q♥ falls under it. North throws the Q♠. Us 3: the hand is made.
  7. Trick 5. You lead the A♠. Nobody has a trump left; West throws the A♦, North the K♦, East the Q♦. Us 4.
  8. Score. The makers took 4 of 5 tricks: 1 point to your team. One more trick would have been a march and 2 points.

Computer players

  • Easy calls trump only on big hands, never goes alone, and plays loosely.
  • Normal bids soundly and plays solid cards: it knows the bowers, keeps track of what has been played, leads trump when its team called it, and doesn’t waste cards on tricks its partner is already winning.
  • Expert bids the same way and a little bolder, then tries out each card it could play many times against likely hands for the other players and picks the one that does best.

The Game speed setting (Relaxed, Normal, Fast, Turbo) changes how long the computers pause before they play, so you can follow along.

Tips and strategy

  1. Count trumps with the left bower. Three trumps, especially with a bower, is usually enough to call. An off-suit Ace makes it much better.
  2. Think about who gets the up-card. Ordering up gives the dealer a trump. That’s good when the dealer is your partner and costly when it’s an opponent.
  3. Try “next” in round two. If everyone passed the up-card, the other suit of the same colour is called next. It is often a good call for the player on the dealer’s left: the dealer chose not to pick up that colour, and the card that was turned down is out of play.
  4. Lead trump when your team made it. Pulling the defenders’ trumps protects your Aces later. As a defender, lead off-suit Aces instead.
  5. Don’t trump your partner’s winner, unless an opponent who plays after you could trump it, as in trick 2 of the example.
  6. Throw your lowest card when you can’t win a trick, and keep your Aces and trumps.
  7. Short suits help. With no cards in a suit you can trump it. When you’re the dealer and pick up, discard to leave yourself void in a suit.
  8. Remember the bowers. Note when each Jack of trump’s colour is played. Once both bowers are gone, the trump Ace is the boss.
  9. Go alone with the top trumps (the right bower plus two or three more high trumps) or with trumps and off-suit Aces. Four points can end a game fast.
  10. Watch the score. Near the end, a risky call that gets euchred can hand the other team the game; a march or a loner can win it outright.

Quick reference

Deck and deal

24 cards, 9 to Ace. Five each; four to the kitty, top card face up. Deal moves left every hand.

Bidding

Round 1: order up the up-card or pass (dealer picks it up and discards). Round 2: name another suit or pass. All pass twice: deal again (or stick the dealer).

Trump order

Right bower (J of trump), left bower (other J of that colour), A, K, Q, 10, 9. Other suits: A, K, Q, J, 10, 9.

Tricks

Dealer’s left leads first. Follow suit if you can (left bower is trump). Highest trump wins, else highest of the suit led. Winner leads.

Scoring

Makers 3–4 tricks: 1. All 5: 2. Alone all 5: 4. Makers under 3: defenders 2.

Winning

First team to 10 (or 5 or 15).

Glossary

Up-card
The kitty’s top card, turned face up after the deal. Its suit is offered as trump in round one.
Kitty
The four cards not dealt. Only the up-card is ever used.
Order up
In round one, make the up-card’s suit trump. The dealer gets the card.
Pick it up
What the dealer says when they order up the up-card themselves.
Name trump
In round two, choose a suit other than the turned-down one.
Trump
The suit chosen for the hand. Any trump beats any card of another suit.
Right bower
The Jack of trump: the highest card.
Left bower
The other Jack of trump’s colour: the second-highest card, and a member of the trump suit for the hand.
Makers
The team that chose trump.
Defenders
The other team.
March
The makers take all five tricks: 2 points.
Euchre
The makers take fewer than three tricks: 2 points to the defenders.
Go alone (loner)
The caller plays without their partner, who sits out. All five tricks score 4.
Stick the dealer
An option where the dealer must name trump if everyone else passed twice.
Follow suit
Play a card of the suit that was led. Required when you can.
Void
Having no cards of a suit, so you may trump it or throw anything.
