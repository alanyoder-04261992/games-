Coastal Dogfight

Take off from a coastal airfield, beat the raiders, bombers, convoys and battleships that come in from the sea, then land your aircraft back on the runway.

  • Pilots1 alone, or up to 4 online
  • Aircraft7 from the 1940s, 3 jets
  • Campaign10 missions
  • Missions6 kinds, by day or night
  • Plays withtouch, keyboard and mouse, gamepad

Goal of the game

You are a fighter pilot on a 20 km stretch of coast in 1943 (or, if you pick a jet, its modern twin). Every mission follows the same shape: take off, do the job, come home and land. The job is to shoot down waves of raiders, sink a battleship, wreck a convoy, stop an ammunition train or turn back a bomber raid.

A mission is won when the job is done and you have landed and stopped on the airfield. You then get a debrief with your score and a grade from A to D. Online, you fly with up to three friends: together against the raiders in co-op, or against each other in a free-for-all where the first pilot to the kill target wins.

Your aircraft

The menu's Choose your aircraft list has ten aircraft in two eras: seven from the 1940s (five fighters, a two-seat fighter and a bomber) and three modern jets. Each card shows a picture, the top speed, guns, missiles and bombs, four bars and two fun facts. The bars compare the aircraft with the others of the same era: turn, speed, climb and guns.

What makes each one different

  • Kestrel: an elliptical wing that turns tightly. All six guns meet at one point 300 m ahead.
  • Falke: small wing and big engine. It out-climbs every other 1940s fighter; its cannon fires through the propeller hub.
  • Ranger: a bare-metal escort with the most fuel of the propeller fighters and six heavy machine guns.
  • Swift: feather-light with the slowest stall (about 75 mph), so it turns inside anything, but it breaks easily.
  • Thunder: a heavy fighter-bomber. Eight heavy machine guns, four 500 kg bombs, and it can limp home on a shot-up engine.
  • Warden: a two-seater. Your gunner, in a turret behind you, fires on his own at enemies within about 700 m above and behind you, but not through his own propeller or tail. A raider straight ahead or right underneath is yours.
  • Brute: a twin-engine bomber with a glazed nose, a top turret and a tail gunner who fire on their own, and ten bombs in the bay. No missiles or rockets.
  • Jets (Lancer, Talon, Striker): boost becomes an afterburner that never runs out but burns fuel fast; fly-by-wire stops a hard pull at 9 g (7.3 g in the Striker); a transonic drag rise holds the fighters to about 740 mph. They have tricycle gear and a rotary cannon. The Striker is slow for a jet, with very little afterburner push, but it takes a lot of punishment.
Raiders fly the other side's fighters of your era. Pick a Kestrel, Ranger, Thunder or Warden and you meet Falkes and Swifts; pick a Falke, Swift or the Brute and you meet Kestrels, Rangers, Thunders and Wardens. Pick a jet and you meet jets: the Lancer and Striker meet Talons, the Talon meets Lancers.

The HUD

Everything you need is drawn over the view. On a computer it looks like this; on a phone the corner map, damage diagram and horizon move to the top left and the flight data runs along the bottom in two lines.

  1. Heading tape. Your compass heading, with ticks for HOME (green), the objective (gold) and your next waypoint (cyan).
  2. Mission line. What to do now: the wave and how many are left, the battleship's hull, targets left, or how far the train is from the depot.
  3. Warnings flash in red: STALL, PULL UP, FIRE, LOW FUEL, FUEL OUT, LEAVING SECTOR, GEAR SPEED, and MISSILE · BREAK HARD when one is coming for you.
  4. Score and kills. Online, the scoreboard of every pilot sits underneath.
  5. Corner map. A heading-up radar over the terrain (enemies red, friends green), a map of the whole sector, or nothing. Switch with M, Y on a gamepad, or on the big map.
  6. Gun sight straight ahead of your nose. White ticks flash on it when you score a hit. In mouse aim a small white circle shows where you are steering.
  7. Enemy brackets in red (a brighter orange-red for an ace), with the range and the height difference (▲ above you, ▼ below).
  8. Lead diamond. On the nearest enemy within 1.5 km in front of you: where to aim so your rounds meet it.
  9. Friends in green, with name and height. Amber brackets mean a friend you are allowed to shoot (free play with friendly fire on).
  10. Off-screen arrow pointing to an aircraft behind or beside you, with its distance in km.
  11. HOME marker, shown when you need it: heading back to land, short of fuel, damaged, out of ammunition, or with the gear down near the field.
  12. Flight data: speed (IAS, mph), altitude (m), climb rate, g, throttle, boost tank (WEP, or A/B on a jet), gear, flaps and fuel.
  13. Artificial horizon: blue sky above, brown earth below. It reads INVERTED when you are upside down.
  14. Damage diagram: each part of your aircraft turns from green to amber to red as it is hit, and black when it is gone.
  15. Weapons left: rounds for each gun type, missiles (MSL), flares (FLR), rockets (RKT) and bombs.

Other things that appear when needed: the missile lock square, a gold bomb sight on the ground, a red arrow to an incoming missile, the landing guide, GEAR UP · PRESS G TO LAND when you are low and slow near the field with the gear up, REARMING, BAIL OUT, a health bar for a named ace, your wingmen's orders, ALT LOCK and FORMATION. At high g the view greys out (blackout from about +6.2 g) or reddens (redout below about −2.4 g).

A sortie, step by step

The menu has two launch buttons. Full sortie · take off puts you on the runway with the engine idling and the brakes on. Quick fight · start airborne puts you in the air at about 1,300 m with the gear up and 90 % throttle, and the first raiders arrive within seconds. Either way the sortie ends with a landing.

  1. Take off. Open the throttle all the way (Shift, the slider or the right stick). Keep straight with the rudder and ease back on the stick once you have flying speed. 1940s aircraft sit on a tail wheel; the jets have a nose wheel.
  2. Climb out. When you are 70 m up, "Airborne. Raise the gear and climb". Press G (GEAR). The first wave arrives 14 seconds later, and leaving the gear down above about 257 mph (336 mph in a fighter jet) flashes GEAR SPEED.
  3. Fight the waves. Raiders come in from the sea. When a wave is down the next arrives 9 seconds later. The Standard setting sends 2 raiders, then 3, then more; later waves have more veterans, and the last raider of the last wave is an ace.
  4. Head home. "All raiders down. Return to base and land." Follow the green HOME tick on the compass.
  5. Land. Lower the gear, follow the landing guide down to the runway and brake to a stop on the airfield.
  6. Debrief. Score, kills, accuracy, rounds fired, damage taken, flight time, how good the landing was and a grade. Fly again or Change aircraft.

Rearm, refuel, repair

Land and stop anywhere on the airfield for 3 seconds (REARMING · 3, 2, 1). You get full fuel, guns, missiles, flares, rockets and bombs, and every part is repaired. You can do this in the middle of any mission.

Shot down? Bail out

When your aircraft is going down the HUD flashes BAIL OUT. Press J (BAIL, or R3) before the wreck hits the ground. You fall clear, the parachute opens a few hundred metres up, and a car or a boat picks you up. In single player you are then back in the air in a fresh aircraft for −100 points and the mission goes on. If the wreck hits the ground with you in it, the mission is over. Half the time you are thrown clear when your aircraft explodes. You can also bail out of a damaged aircraft at any time (press twice from a healthy one).

Online, a pilot who is shot down is back in the air after 6 seconds; one who bailed out is back once the parachute lands.

On the naval, ground and train missions, enemy fighters scramble to defend the target, two at a time, up to three times. Flak bursts near you hurt, so keep moving. Trucks fall to guns; tanks need cannon, rockets or bombs; fuel tanks go up in a fireball. A wagon that blows up on the train can set off the cars next to it.

Weapons for the job

  • Bombs: every aircraft carries them on every mission. B in the air (BOMB, LT). The bomber drops them in pairs.
  • Rockets: K, RKT or L3 fires a pair. They burn for a second, then fall like shells and burst with a blast about 14 m across, on aircraft, vehicles, wagons, the battleship or the ground.
  • Guns: good on trucks and wagons, weak on armour. Cannon fire helps a little against the battleship.

Time of day

Day, Sunset or Night. At night the town, the runway lights and the lighthouse are lit, tracers glow brighter, and searchlights sweep the sky round the targets (the battleship has two). A searchlight that catches you holds you until you turn hard out of it, and while you are lit the flak is far more accurate.

Named aces

The last wave of a single-player dogfight brings a named ace: Viktor Kessel, the Black Heron (black with yellow trim), Ilse Brandt, the Red Wasp (red) or, against jets, Arkady Volk, the Grey Ghost (grey). An ace has nose art, a health bar at the top of the HUD and about 1.8 times the toughness of a normal aircraft. It notices you on its tail quickly and scissors, reversing its turn every second or so. Badly hit, it makes a last stand: no more dodging, full power and every missile it has. Shooting one down is worth +1,000.

Wingmen

Choose none, one or two (Mack and Dutch) in the menu. They cover you to begin with. T sends them after your target (the locked one, or the nearest ahead), Y brings them back to cover you, and U sends them at the ground target with guns, rockets and bombs. An enemy your wingman or gunner is shooting at gets a green dashed ring and a tag (MACK, DUTCH, GUNNER).

Worked example. A one-wave dogfight on Normal sends a rookie and a veteran: 250 points, so the best total is 250 + 620 = 870 (a dogfight adds 620 for a perfect landing, coming home and accuracy; other missions add 700). You shoot both down (250), hit with 15 % of your rounds (+60), come home (+200) and make a firm landing (+200): 710 ÷ 870 = 82 %, an A. Thump it down hard instead (+100) and it is 610 ÷ 870 = 70 %, a B.

Online the grade works differently. In co-op: A if you flew in a successful mission and were never shot down, B if you were, C for any kill or ground target, otherwise D. In a free-for-all it follows your place: A for first, B second, C third, D fourth.

Co-op

  • The round starts in free play: no raiders, just the sky. The menu offers a Free play card and the missions.
  • Anyone can start a mission (Dogfight, Naval strike, Ground attack, Train busting, Bomber intercept) as they launch or from the pause menu. Everyone else sees "Ana started Naval strike — Join mission ✕" for 30 seconds. Join even partway through, or stay in free play. Different pilots can run different missions at the same time.
  • Everyone gets the points for what they destroy, and every member gets +500 when the mission is won.
  • In free play, with friendly fire on, your rounds can hit another pilot who is also in free play; they show in amber.
  • The host ends the round from the pause menu.

Free-for-all

Every pilot for themselves. Computer pilots fly the empty seats, at the table's computer player level (Rookie, Veteran or Ace). The first to the kill target wins; the host can end it early.

At the end

The debrief shows the scoreboard (kills and times shot down). The Rematch button starts a new round with the same pilots, seats and room code.

With another person at the table the game can't stop for you: the pause menu stays open while your aircraft holds level flight. With only computer pilots at the table, pausing freezes everything.

Techniques

Shooting: lead the target

  • Put the gun sight on the diamond, not on the aircraft, and fire short bursts. White ticks on the sight mean hits.
  • Fire from about 300 m. Further out, rounds spread and drop; closer, you risk flying through the debris.
  • Every second or third round is a tracer, so you can see where the stream goes and walk it onto the target.
  • Rotary cannons empty fast: the Lancer's 510 rounds last about ten seconds of fire.

Missiles and flares

  • Auto lock (default) works in a cone about 24° round your nose and takes 0.8 s. Manual lock needs the target within about 6° for 1.6 s. Targets lock between 120 m and 2.6 km away.
  • A missile fired without a lock flies straight. A guided one burns for 3 seconds, leads its target, can only turn so hard, and bursts when it passes close.
  • Each flare press drops a pair. A missile chasing you within 2.6 km has a 60 % chance of going for them. Turn hard at the same time.
  • Land and stop on the airfield to get your missiles and flares back.

Energy fighting

  • Keep your speed up. Pulling hard bleeds speed. When the stall horn sounds, ease off: a stalled wing drops and the nose falls.
  • Height is a bank account. Arrive above the fight and you can always buy speed by diving.
  • Turn inside them. You get about 30 % more lift than the computer's aircraft and a quicker roll, and they notice you on their tail later than you notice them.
  • Combat flaps. Lower the flaps (V or FLAP) in the air, bank and pull: you come round at up to about 60° a second at any speed, jets included, against about 15–18° a second for a Kestrel without them. It costs speed and fades near the stall, so raise the flaps afterwards to get the speed back. Computer pilots never get this.
  • Boost. Hold Shift at full throttle (or drag into the red top of the slider) for about 18 seconds of extra power. It recharges when you ease off. A jet's afterburner doesn't run out but burns fuel about three times as fast.
  • Mind the g. Above about +11.5 g held, or below −5.5 g, the wings are damaged. Fly-by-wire jets stop at their limit by themselves.
  • Against an ace, don't sit straight behind: it scissors. Stay a little high and cut across as it reverses.

Landing

  1. Line up from a distance. Turn so the runway is ahead, 5 to 7 km out. Either end works: the guide always uses the near end.
  2. Gear down, speed back. Lower the gear (G) and, if you like, the flaps (V). Aim for the approach speed the guide shows: 1.3 times your stall speed. "TOO FAST" means over 1.25 times that, "TOO SLOW" under 0.92 times.
  3. Fly the gates. Keep the readout green: ON THE GLIDE PATH (within 25 m), ON THE CENTRELINE (within 20 m), SPEED ✓.
  4. Flare and touch down. Just above the runway, ease the throttle off and raise the nose a little so you sink gently onto the wheels.
  5. Brake to a stop on the airfield (B, or LT; zero throttle on the ground brakes too). The mission ends once you have stopped.
A touchdown is survivable with the gear down, sinking slower than 6.5 m/s, banked less than 16°, the nose not pointing down, on flat ground. Forgot the gear? A gentle belly landing (under 3.5 m/s and about 160 mph) is survivable too, but it ends the sortie with the engine wrecked.

Ground attack

  • Come in from the side of the target the flak isn't facing, and don't fly straight and level near it for long.
  • Release bombs when the gold sight sits on the target. A shallow dive at a steady speed gives a sight that moves slowly and predictably.
  • Use rockets on the train: one exploding wagon can set off its neighbours. Stop the locomotive to stop the train.
  • Send your wingmen in with U, and rearm at the airfield between passes.

Finding your way

N (or MAP) opens a map of the whole world: the arctic north, the desert south, mountains to the west and islands out to sea in the east. Tap or click to add up to six waypoints, then Fly route and an autopilot flies them, holding your height but keeping 250 m above the ground ahead. Any stick input takes over again; I (or ROUTE) switches it back on. In single player the game pauses while the map is open. You may fly up to 27 km from the centre; LEAVING SECTOR warns you, and further out the aircraft turns back.

Boost (WEP)
War emergency power: about 18 seconds of extra power at full throttle, recharging when you ease off.
Combat flaps
Flaps lowered in a fight for a very tight, speed-bleeding turn.
Convergence
The point, 300 m ahead, where all your guns are aimed to meet.
Deflection
Aiming ahead of a target that is crossing your nose; the lead diamond shows how much.
Flak
Anti-aircraft fire from the ground or from ships.
Flares
Hot decoys you drop to pull a missile away.
g
How hard you are pulling, in multiples of your weight.
Glide path
The 3.5° slope down to the runway the landing gates mark.
IAS
Indicated airspeed, in mph: what your wings feel.
Lead diamond
The white diamond showing where to aim to hit the nearest enemy ahead.
Raider
An enemy aircraft sent against you in a mission.
RTB
Return to base: head home and land.
Sortie
One flight, from take-off to landing.
Stall
When the wing is asked for too much angle and stops lifting. Ease off the stick and keep your speed up.
Wave
One group of raiders. The next comes 9 seconds after the last is shot down.
